Safe Room Temperature for Your Infant&#x27;s Sleep

Maintaining the correct room temperature is a critical factor in creating a safe sleep environment for your baby. A room that is too warm can increase the risk of overheating, which is linked to a higher risk of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) . The good news is that keeping your baby&#x27;s room at a safe and comfortable temperature is simple to manage.

The Recommended Temperature Range

The safest room temperature for an infant to sleep in is between 16°C and 20°C (60.8°F °F) . Many health organizations, including the NHS and The Lullaby Trust, recommend aiming for the middle of this range, around 18°C (65°F), which is considered just right . The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) advises keeping the room at a temperature that is &quot;comfortable for an adult&quot; . To easily monitor this, place a room thermometer in your baby&#x27;s sleep area . This is far more reliable than guessing based on how warm you feel.

Why This Temperature Matters

Overheating is a known risk factor for SIDS, and babies are more sensitive to temperature changes than adults . A room that is too hot, combined with too much bedding or clothing, can cause a baby to overheat . Therefore, it is always better for a baby to be slightly cooler rather than too hot .

How to Dress Your Baby for Sleep

Dressing your baby appropriately for the room temperature is key to preventing overheating. A good rule of thumb is to dress your baby in one more layer than you would wear to bed yourself .

Here is a simple guide based on the room temperature:

Room Temperature 16–20°C: A nappy, a vest, and a babygrow (sleepsuit) are usually sufficient .

Room Temperature Below 16°C: You may need to add an extra layer of clothing or use a higher tog sleep sack .

Other Important Safety Tips:

Never put a hat on your baby for sleep indoors, as babies lose excess heat through their heads and a hat can cause overheating .

Do not use duvets, quilts, pillows, or loose blankets in the cot, as these can increase the risk of suffocation and overheating .

Instead of loose blankets, use a well-fitting, sleeveless baby sleeping bag (sleep sack) with the correct TOG rating for the room temperature . For instance, a 2.5 TOG sleep sack is suitable for rooms around 16–20°C .

How to Check if Your Baby is Too Warm

Your baby&#x27;s hands and feet may naturally feel cool, which is normal . The best way to check if they are too warm is to feel the back of their neck or their tummy . If their skin feels hot or sweaty, they are too warm, and you should remove a layer of clothing or bedding . Other signs of overheating include flushed or red cheeks, damp hair, or rapid breathing .
