Screen Time Before Bed for a 9-Month-Old: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

For a 9-month-old, screen time before bed is not recommended. A 9-month-old learns best through face-to-face interaction, movement, and play, not through screens . The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) advises avoiding screen time entirely for babies under 18 months, with the sole exception of supervised video chatting with family and friends . The World Health Organization (WHO) and other health bodies echo this, recommending no sedentary screen time for infants under 1 year old .

🥱 Why Screens Before Bed Are Disruptive

Screen time close to bedtime can be a significant &quot;causal disruptive factor&quot; for infant sleep . Even having a television on in the background can negatively impact a child&#x27;s development by disrupting parent-child interaction and the baby&#x27;s ability to focus .

Impact on Melatonin: Artificial lighting from screens can stimulate a baby&#x27;s brain and reduce the production of melatonin, the hormone that helps regulate sleep-wake cycles . However, a 2025 experimental study found that watching a story on a tablet did not suppress melatonin production in young children (15–24 months) any differently than reading a picture book . While the science on blue light&#x27;s effect is evolving, the consensus remains that a screen-free hour before bedtime is a safe and effective practice .

🌙 Practical Steps for a Screen-Free Bedtime Routine

Instead of screen time, you should focus on establishing a consistent, calming bedtime routine:

Establish a Routine: A simple, predictable sequence of events—like a warm bath, putting on pajamas, reading a story, and a lullaby—signals that sleep is approaching .

Create a Screen-Free Zone: Keep televisions, tablets, and phones out of the bedroom. The bedroom should be a &quot;no-screen zone&quot; .

Use a Red Nightlight: If your child is afraid of the dark, a red-colored nightlight is best, as it doesn&#x27;t disrupt melatonin production. An orange or amber-colored light is a good alternative .

Be a Role Model: Your own device use matters. &quot;Parental technoference&quot;—when a parent&#x27;s device use interrupts interaction—can lead to more negative reactions from a baby . Setting aside time for undivided attention is crucial for your child&#x27;s development.

By creating a screen-free wind-down routine, you help your 9-month-old develop healthy sleep habits that can last a lifetime.
