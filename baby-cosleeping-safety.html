Co-Sleeping Safety for Your Baby

Co-sleeping—sharing a sleeping space with your baby—can mean different things to different families. The term is often used to describe both room-sharing (the baby sleeps in the same room but on a separate surface) and bed-sharing (the baby sleeps on the same surface as an adult). Understanding the difference is crucial for your baby&#x27;s safety.

Room-Sharing: Safe and Recommended

Room-sharing means your baby sleeps in the same room as you, but in their own separate, safe sleep surface. This is the recommended practice for a safe co-sleeping arrangement.

The medical consensus:

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends that infants sleep in the same room as their parents—but on a separate sleep surface—for at least the first 6 months of life, and ideally for the first year.

This arrangement reduces the risk of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) by up to 50%.

Room-sharing makes nighttime feeding and comforting easier while keeping your baby close but safe.

Bed-Sharing: Not Recommended

Bed-sharing means that the baby sleeps on the same surface as an adult—in a bed, on a couch, or in a chair. This practice is not recommended by the AAP, CDC, and other major health organizations for infants under 12 months.

Why bed-sharing is dangerous:

The risk of suffocation increases more than 60 times when an infant shares a sleep surface with an adult.

Adult beds are not designed for infants and pose risks of entrapment, falls, and suffocation from soft bedding.

Most SIDS deaths occur in the first 6 months of life, making this a critical period for safe sleep practices.

What to Do

If you are co-sleeping, the safest option is to move your baby to a separate sleep surface in the same room. This can be a:

Crib or bassinet with a firm, flat mattress and fitted sheet

Play yard or portable crib

Bedside sleeper attached to the parent&#x27;s bed

Safe sleep essentials:

Place your baby on their back for every sleep.

Keep soft objects, pillows, blankets, and toys out of the sleep area.

Avoid overheating; check for sweating or a hot chest.

Never sleep with your baby on a couch, armchair, or waterbed.

Avoid bed-sharing if you smoke, drink alcohol, or take medications that make you sleepy.
