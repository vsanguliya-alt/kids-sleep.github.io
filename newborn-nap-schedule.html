Newborn Nap Schedule: A Guide for Parents

Newborns have very different sleep patterns compared to older babies and children. In the early weeks, they don't yet have a regular day-night rhythm and sleep in short bursts, waking frequently to feed.

How Much Sleep Does a Newborn Need?

Newborns typically sleep in short periods of 1 to 4 hours and are awake for about 1 to 2 hours at a time. Because they need to feed often, their sleep is distributed fairly evenly across the day and night.

What to Expect in the First Weeks

In the first month, you can expect your baby to sleep for about 16 hours a day in total. These sleep periods are usually spaced evenly between feedings. During this time, there is no fixed schedule; naps can last anywhere from 3 to 4 hours.

Your baby's tummy is tiny, so they need to feed frequently—roughly every 2 to 3 hours. This feeding requirement naturally determines their sleep schedule in the early weeks.

Your baby's internal clock (circadian rhythm) is not yet developed, so they haven't learned to distinguish day from night. You can help them adjust by keeping daytime feeds and interactions bright and lively, while keeping nighttime feeds quiet and dark.

Watching for Sleep Cues

It's important to watch for sleep cues. After being awake for about 1 to 2 hours, most newborns show signs that they are ready for sleep. Look for:

Yawning

Eye-rubbing

Fussiness

Looking away from stimulation

When you notice these signs, it's a good time to offer a nap. This can help prevent your baby from becoming overtired, which can make it harder for them to settle down to sleep.

A Note on Safe Sleep for Newborns

During all sleep periods—both naps and nighttime—safe sleep is crucial. Always place your baby on their back to sleep. Use a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet, and keep the sleep area clear of soft objects, pillows, and loose bedding to ensure a safe environment for your newborn.
