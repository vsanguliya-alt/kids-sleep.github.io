The Ideal Room Temperature for Your 18-Month-Old&#x27;s Sleep

Maintaining a comfortable and safe room temperature is an important part of helping your toddler sleep well and safely. While much of the official guidance focuses on babies under 12 months to reduce the risk of SIDS, the principles of avoiding overheating and ensuring a comfortable sleep environment apply to toddlers as well.

The Recommended Room Temperature

For safe and comfortable sleep, experts recommend keeping your child&#x27;s bedroom at a temperature between 16°C and 20°C. This is based on guidance from the NHS, The Lullaby Trust, and other health organizations .

While this 16°C range is universally recommended for babies under one, it continues to be a sound and comfortable guideline for an 18-month-old. Keeping the room comfortably cool helps prevent overheating, which can disrupt sleep and cause discomfort.

Why This Temperature Matters

The main reason for this guidance is that overheating is a known risk factor for Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) in babies under 12 months . For older toddlers, the concern shifts more towards general comfort and safety.

Overheating can disturb sleep: A room that is too warm can make your child restless, sweaty, and uncomfortable, leading to more night wakings.

Prevents heat-related illness: Young children are more vulnerable to heat-related illness as they are less able to regulate their body temperature. Signs of overheating include flushed skin, rapid breathing, and lethargy .

Promotes safe sleep: A cool room is a safe room. Keeping the temperature in check is a simple way to ensure your child is not too hot during sleep.

How to Manage the Temperature

Use a room thermometer: This is the most reliable way to know the temperature of your child&#x27;s room, rather than just guessing.

Dress your child appropriately for the room temperature: Use lightweight bedding and pajamas, and add or remove layers as needed.

Monitor for signs of being too hot: Feel the back of your child&#x27;s neck or their tummy. If they feel clammy or sweaty, they are too warm. Red cheeks and damp hair are also signs of overheating .
