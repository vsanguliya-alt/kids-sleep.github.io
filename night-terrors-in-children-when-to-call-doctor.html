Bedtime Routine Tips for Young Children

Why a Bedtime Routine Matters

A consistent bedtime routine is one of the most effective ways to help your child get the sleep they need. Sleep supports physical growth, brain development, learning, and emotional wellbeing . Without enough sleep, children may have trouble paying attention, become overly active, and face challenges regulating their emotions .

Children aged 3 to 5 need about 10 to 13 hours of sleep per day, including naps . Setting up a predictable routine helps their body and brain know it's time to wind down.

Key Steps for a Successful Bedtime Routine

Keep it consistent. Doing the same activities in the same order each night helps children know what to expect . A simple routine might include a bath, brushing teeth, putting on pajamas, reading a story, and saying goodnight .

Start winding down early. About an hour before bedtime, turn off electronic devices like tablets, TVs, and phones. The blue light from screens can interfere with melatonin, the hormone that helps children feel sleepy . Use this time for calming activities like reading or quiet conversation instead.

Make the bedroom sleep-friendly. Keep the room cool, dark, and quiet . A dim nightlight with a warm glow (red or orange) is less likely to disrupt sleep than bright white or blue light . Remove televisions and other screens from the bedroom .

Put your child to bed drowsy but awake. This helps them learn to fall asleep on their own . If they wake during the night, they're more likely to settle themselves back to sleep.

Avoid stimulants before bed. Limit active play, scary stories, and caffeinated drinks (like tea, coffee, or soda) in the hours before bedtime . Avoid putting children to bed with a bottle, as this can cause tooth decay and ear infections .

Handling Bedtime Resistance

If your child calls out or gets out of bed, stay calm and consistent. Offer quick reassurance, but avoid prolonged cuddling or bringing them into your bed . Some parents find that calmly returning the child to bed with a brief statement—like "It's time to sleep"—helps reinforce the routine .

Stick With It

It may take several nights for a new routine to stick . Consistency and patience are key. Healthy sleep habits developed in childhood can benefit children well into adulthood . If sleep problems persist or you have concerns, talk to your pediatrician.
