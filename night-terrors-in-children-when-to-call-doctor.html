Night Terrors in Children: When to Call the Doctor

Night terrors (sleep terrors) are episodes of screaming, intense fear, and flailing while still asleep . They occur most often in children between ages 3 and 8, though they may happen from age 1 to 12 . Unlike nightmares, children usually don&#x27;t remember the episode the next morning and cannot be comforted or awakened during it .

✅ Normal Night Terrors

VERIFIED: Occasional night terrors are not usually a cause for concern . Most children outgrow them by their teenage years .

VERIFIED: Common triggers include:

Being very tired or unwell Stress, anxiety, or emotional conflict Fever VERIFIED: To help during an episode:

Stay calm and wait for it to pass Do not try to wake the child — they may become more upset and not recognize you Keep the child safe from injury (e.g., block stairways, lock windows) ⚠️ When to Call the Doctor

Contact your pediatrician if night terrors :

Occur often or regularly — episodes happen more than occasionally

Disrupt sleep on a regular basis for the child or family

Last longer than 30 minutes

Lead to safety concerns or dangerous behavior during an episode

Result in daytime symptoms — extreme sleepiness or problems with daily activities

VERIFIED: Also seek medical advice if:

The child has drooling, jerking, or stiffening during an episode (may indicate seizures) Night terrors start after age 5 or continue after age 12 The child has daytime fears or you feel family stress may be a factor You have other concerns or the cause is unclear SPORNO: Treatment is rarely needed but may include addressing stress, anticipatory awakening (waking the child 15 minutes before the usual episode time), or in very rare cases, medication .
