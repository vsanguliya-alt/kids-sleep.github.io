Night Terrors in Children: When to Call the Doctor

Night terrors (sleep terrors) are a sleep disorder in which a person quickly wakes from sleep in a terrified state . While alarming for parents to witness, they are usually harmless and most children outgrow them. This guide explains what night terrors are and when to seek medical advice.

Understanding Night Terrors

Night terrors are a type of parasomnia—a disturbing behavior that occurs during sleep. They usually happen during the first third of the night, often between midnight and 2 a.m., as your child transitions from the deepest stage of sleep to a lighter one .

During an episode, your child may sit up in bed, scream or cry, and appear scared but unaware of your presence . They may also sweat, breathe heavily, have a rapid heart rate, and be difficult or impossible to comfort or wake . Episodes typically last from a few seconds to a few minutes, though they can last longer . Unlike with nightmares, children usually have no memory of the event the next morning .

When to Call the Doctor

Occasional night terrors are not usually a cause for concern, and you can simply mention them at a routine well-child exam . However, you should contact your pediatrician if night terrors:

Occur often or frequently .

Regularly disrupt sleep for the child or the family .

Last longer than 30 minutes .

Lead to safety concerns or injury .

Result in daytime symptoms of extreme sleepiness or problems with daily activities .

Are accompanied by other symptoms, such as drooling, jerking, or stiffening (which could indicate seizures) .

Cause daytime fears or if you feel family stress may be a factor .

Seek emergency care immediately if a night terror leads to dangerous behavior, such as running around the house or if your child is injured during an episode .

What to Do During an Episode

During a night terror, do not try to wake your child, as it may disorient them. Instead, stay nearby to ensure their safety and gently guide them back to bed if they get up . With time, most children outgrow night terrors entirely .
