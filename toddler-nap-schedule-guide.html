Toddler Nap Schedule in Children 0: Complete Parent Guide

Naps are a vital part of a toddler's daily routine. They are not just a break for parents; they are essential for a child's healthy growth, development, and mood. A good nap schedule ensures your child gets the rest they need without interfering with nighttime sleep. This guide provides evidence-based information to help you navigate the changing landscape of toddler naps.

Why Naps Matter

Naps offer several crucial benefits for young children. Sleep, both at night and during the day, is critical for brain development . It helps consolidate learning and memory, and is a key time for the body to rest and restore energy . Studies have shown that children who nap regularly tend to have longer attention spans and better nighttime sleep . Properly timed naps also prevent overtiredness, which can make it harder for a child to fall asleep and stay asleep at night .

How Much Sleep Does a Toddler Need?

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends that toddlers aged 1 to 2 years get 11 to 14 hours of sleep in a 24-hour period, which includes both nighttime sleep and naps .

Toddler Nap Schedules by Age

The number and duration of naps a toddler needs change as they grow. Here is a general guide to what you can expect.

12 to 18 Months: Transitioning from Two Naps to One

Typical Nap Count: 1 to 2 naps per day.

Nap Length: 1 to 2 hours per nap.

Most toddlers transition from two naps to one between 12 and 18 months of age . During this transition, your child may need a morning and an afternoon nap on some days, and only one nap on others . A good signal that they are ready for this transition is when they start consistently fighting the morning nap or taking a very long time to fall asleep for it.

Sample 2-Nap Schedule:

7:00 AM: Wake up

9:30 AM: Morning nap (approx. 1 hour)

1:30 PM: Afternoon nap (approx. 1.5 hours)

7:30 PM: Bedtime routine begins

8:00 PM: Sleep

Sample 1-Nap Schedule:

7:00 AM: Wake up

12:30 PM: Afternoon nap (1.5 to 2.5 hours)

7:30 PM: Bedtime routine begins

8:00 PM: Sleep

18 Months to 3 Years: The One-Nap Phase

Typical Nap Count: 1 nap per day.

Nap Length: 1.5 to 2.5 hours.

By around 18 months, most toddlers have settled into a consistent one-nap schedule . This single nap is typically taken in the early afternoon, starting around 12:30 PM or 1:00 PM . The goal is to have the nap end with enough time before bedtime—ideally at least 3 to 4 hours—so your toddler is tired enough to fall asleep at night.

Sample 1-Nap Schedule:

7:00 AM: Wake up

1:00 PM: Afternoon nap (1.5 to 2 hours)

8:00 PM: Bedtime routine begins

8:30 PM: Sleep

Signs Your Toddler is Ready to Drop a Nap

Knowing when to reduce or eliminate a nap can be tricky. Common signs that your toddler is ready for a nap transition include:

Taking 30 minutes or more to fall asleep at naptime .

Consistently fighting or refusing a nap .

Having trouble falling asleep at bedtime or waking up very early in the morning after a nap .

Not seeming tired at their usual naptime.

Tips for a Successful Nap Schedule

1. Maintain a Consistent Routine

A predictable, shortened version of your bedtime routine can help signal that it's time to sleep. This might include a story, a song, or quiet cuddling . Consistency in timing is key—aim to put your child down for a nap at the same time each day .

2. Create a Sleep-Friendly Environment

A dark, quiet, and cool room is ideal for naps . Consider using blackout curtains to minimize light and a white noise machine to block out household sounds .

3. Learn Your Child's Sleepy Cues

Watch for signs of sleepiness such as rubbing eyes, yawning, fussiness, or staring into space . Put your child down for a nap before they become overtired, which can actually make it harder for them to fall asleep .

4. Put Your Child in the Crib Drowsy but Awake

This helps them learn to self-soothe and fall asleep independently, which is a skill that will serve them well during night wakings .

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

While some fluctuation in nap schedules is normal, you should consult your pediatrician if:

Sleep disruption is severe and persistent, and it starts affecting your child's daytime behavior and development .

Your child is consistently unable to fall asleep before 9 or 10 PM, despite your efforts to adjust their nap schedule .

You suspect a medical issue, such as your child snoring loudly and seeming excessively sleepy during the day, which could be a sign of sleep apnea .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my toddler's nap schedule?

Call your pediatrician if sleep disruption is severe and persistent, affecting your child's daytime behavior and development. Also, seek medical advice if your child consistently cannot fall asleep before 9PM despite efforts to adjust the nap, or if they snore loudly and seem excessively sleepy during the day regardless of nap timing .

Q: At what age do toddlers typically stop napping?

Most children stop napping between the ages of 3 and 5. Some may stop as early as 2, while others might need naps until age 5 .

Q: What home remedies can help a toddler nap?

Maintain a consistent routine, create a dark and quiet sleep environment, watch for sleepy cues, and put your child down "drowsy but awake" to help them learn to self-soothe .

Q: How long should a toddler's nap be?

For toddlers on a one-nap schedule, a nap typically lasts between 1.5 and 2.5 hours . Naps should be long enough to be restorative but not so long that they interfere with nighttime sleep.

Q: When should I transition my toddler to one nap?

Most toddlers transition from two naps to one between 12 and 18 months of age. Signs of readiness include consistently fighting one of the naps or taking a long time to fall asleep for it .

Q: When should I go to the ER for a sleep issue?

Go to the ER immediately if your child has trouble breathing, turns blue or grey, or is very difficult to wake. These are emergency signs. For other sleep concerns, contact your pediatrician.
