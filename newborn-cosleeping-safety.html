Newborn Co-Sleeping Safety: What Every Parent Needs to Know

Co-sleeping—sharing a sleeping space with your newborn—is a practice that requires careful attention to safety. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) makes a clear distinction between room-sharing (safe and recommended) and bed-sharing (dangerous and strongly discouraged).

Room-Sharing: Safe and Recommended

Room-sharing means your baby sleeps in the same room as you, but in their own separate, safe sleep surface. This is the recommended practice for newborns.

The AAP recommends that infants sleep in the same room as their parents—but on a separate sleep surface—for at least the first 6 months, and ideally for the first year.

This arrangement reduces the risk of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) by up to 50%.

Room-sharing makes nighttime feeding and comforting easier while keeping your baby close but safe.

Bed-Sharing: Not Recommended

Bed-sharing means that the baby sleeps on the same surface as an adult—in a bed, on a couch, or in a chair. This practice is not recommended by the AAP and other major health organizations for newborns and infants under 12 months.

Why bed-sharing is dangerous for newborns:

The risk of suffocation increases dramatically when an infant shares a sleep surface with an adult.

Adult beds are not designed for newborns and pose risks of entrapment, falls, and suffocation from soft bedding.

Most SIDS deaths occur in the first 6 months of life, making this a critical period for safe sleep practices.

If You Choose to Bed-Share: Risk Factors to Avoid

The risk of bed-sharing is dramatically higher in certain situations. You should never share a bed with your baby if:

You or your partner smoke.

You or your partner have consumed alcohol or drugs (including any medication that makes you drowsy).

Your baby was premature (born before 37 weeks) or had a low birth weight.

You are overtired or extremely fatigued.

Your baby is under 3 months old.

Furthermore, sleeping with a baby on a sofa or armchair is exceptionally dangerous and strongly linked to a high risk of SIDS and accidental death.

What to Do

The safest option for your newborn is to move your baby to a separate sleep surface in the same room. This can be a:

Crib or bassinet with a firm, flat mattress and fitted sheet.

Play yard or portable crib.

Bedside sleeper attached to the parent&#x27;s bed.

Safe sleep essentials:

Place your baby on their back for every sleep.

Keep soft objects, pillows, blankets, and toys out of the sleep area.

Avoid overheating; check for sweating or a hot chest.
