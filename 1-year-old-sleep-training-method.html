Gentle Sleep Training Methods for Your 1-Year-Old

Teaching your 1-year-old to sleep independently can feel like a challenge, but many toddlers are ready to learn this skill with a consistent and gentle approach. At this age, children are often more independent but may also experience stronger separation anxiety, making a gradual method that offers reassurance particularly effective. While many toddlers show significant progress within 7–10 nights of consistent training, the process can take up to two weeks to fully establish new habits .

Gentle, Parent-Present Methods

The most recommended approaches for a 1-year-old involve staying close to provide comfort while encouraging self-soothing .

The Sleep Lady Shuffle is a gentle, gradual method specifically designed for toddlers. You start by sitting next to your child&#x27;s crib and offering reassurance as they fall asleep. Over several nights, you slowly move your chair farther away until you are outside the room . This method is ideal for easing separation anxiety and builds your child&#x27;s confidence in sleeping alone .

The Camp-Out or Chair Method involves &quot;camping out&quot; next to your child&#x27;s crib. At first, you pat your child gently to sleep instead of picking them up. Once they are used to falling asleep with your presence but less physical touch, you begin moving your sleeping spot further away from the crib each night until you are out of the room . This can take about 2 to 3 weeks but is considered a kind and reassuring approach .

The Pick-Up, Put-Down Method can be effective for toddlers up to 18 months old. You run through your child&#x27;s regular bedtime routine, put them in the crib, and sit quietly next to them. If they cry, you can pick them up and cuddle until they calm down, then place them back in the crib. Stay in the child&#x27;s room until they fall asleep, and over several days, move your chair closer to the door until you are out of the room .

Methods with Controlled Crying

The Ferber Method, also known as &quot;graduated extinction,&quot; involves timed check-ins. You put your child to bed awake, leave the room, and if they cry, you wait a set amount of time (e.g., 3 minutes) before going in to offer brief reassurance without picking them up. You leave again, gradually increasing the wait time between visits . The goal is to teach self-soothing while letting the child know they are not abandoned .

Sleep Training Tips for Success

Regardless of the method you choose, consistency and a calm routine are key to success .

Establish a Consistent Bedtime Routine: A predictable pattern—such as a warm bath, reading a book, and a song—signals to your child that it is time to sleep . Keep this routine under 30 minutes and perform the steps in the same order every night .

Put Your Child Down Drowsy but Awake: Placing your child in their crib while they are calm but still awake helps them practice falling asleep on their own, which is the core skill they need to learn .

Know Your Child&#x27;s Sleep Cues: Put your child down when they first show signs of tiredness, such as rubbing eyes or becoming quiet, to catch their optimal &quot;sleep window&quot; . An overtired child will find it harder to settle.

What to Do When Your Child Cries

When your child cries during sleep training, your response will depend on your chosen method. If you are using a parent-present method like the Shuffle or Chair, you can offer a reassuring word or gentle pat. If you are using the Ferber method, you would wait for the scheduled check-in time. It is common for children to cry initially, as they are learning a new skill .

Try to keep your responses calm, brief, and boring at night. If your child stands up in the crib, avoid turning it into a game by repeatedly laying them back down. Gently pat the mattress and encourage them to lie down on their own, using calm words . If they cry, speak calmly and reassure them, then leave the room. Be consistent and avoid picking them up or giving them a bottle .

When Sleep Training Is Not Working

If sleep training is consistently challenging, consider if your child is unwell, teething, or going through a developmental leap . A common 12-month sleep regression can also cause setbacks. If the method you are using does not seem to be a good fit for your family after a fair trial, it is okay to take a pause and try a different, gentler approach . Trust your instincts; if you are both distressed by the process, it may not be the right time or method for you .
