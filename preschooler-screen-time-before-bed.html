Preschooler Screen Time Before Bed: Why It Matters for Sleep

As a parent of a preschooler, you are likely familiar with the challenge of winding your child down at the end of the day. Screen time—from tablets, smartphones, or televisions—can be a tempting way to occupy a preschooler, but it is important to understand how it affects their sleep.

How Screen Time Disrupts Sleep

The blue light emitted by screens can interfere with the production of melatonin, the hormone that regulates sleep-wake cycles. This makes it harder for your child to fall asleep and stay asleep . Research confirms a clear link: studies show that as screen time increases, sleep problems in preschool children also rise .

The Evidence on Screen Time and Sleep

One study found that removing screens in the hour before bedtime helped toddlers sleep better and wake up less during the night . This is supported by broader research showing that screen use before bed is associated with fewer minutes of sleep and more sleep difficulties in young children .

Recommended Guidelines for Screen Time

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends that children aged 2years should have no more than one hour of high-quality screen time per day . Additionally, the AAP strongly recommends that screens be turned off at least one hour before bedtime and removed from the bedroom entirely .

How to Create a Screen-Free Wind-Down Routine

Instead of screen time, focus on establishing a calming bedtime routine that helps your child settle. A consistent, predictable routine helps signal that it&#x27;s time to sleep . A 20minute routine might include:

A warm bath

Brushing teeth

Reading a story (using a physical book, not a tablet)

Quiet cuddles and conversation What to Do If Screen Time Has Been a Habit

If your child is used to having a screen before bed, a sudden stop might be challenging. Consider these tips:

Gradually reduce screen time: Move the screen time earlier in the evening and reduce the duration gradually.

Replace with a calming activity: Transition from screen time to a bath or reading a book .

Be consistent: A calm, structured bedtime routine is the most effective tool for helping your child get the rest they need. Consistency is key to success .

Involve your child: Let your preschooler choose the book or bedtime activity to give them a sense of control .
