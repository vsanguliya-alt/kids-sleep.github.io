4-Year-Old Nap Schedule: A Guide for Parents

By age 4, your child's sleep needs are changing significantly, and the daytime nap is often a thing of the past. This is a major developmental milestone, but it can also be a challenging transition for both parents and children.

How Much Sleep Does a 4-Year-Old Need?

A 4-year-old needs about 10 to 13 hours of good quality sleep over a 24-hour period . This total sleep time is usually broken down into:

Nighttime sleep: Approximately 10 to 12 hours .

Daytime sleep (nap): Most 4-year-olds do not nap, but some may still need a short catnap .

The End of Naps: What to Expect

By age 4, most children are no longer napping during the day . While some 4-year-olds may still take a short nap, many are ready to drop it entirely . This is a normal developmental transition.

Signs your child may be ready to drop their nap include :

Regularly resisting naps despite appearing tired.

Taking a long time (30+ minutes) to fall asleep for their nap.

Having difficulty falling asleep at night or waking early after a daytime nap.

Skipping naps some days without major behavior issues.

Staying happy and well-rested without a nap.

How to Manage the Transition

Establish a Consistent Schedule

A consistent bedtime and wake-up time is key to ensuring your child gets enough sleep. Most 4-year-olds need about 10hours of sleep per night . A typical schedule may look like:

Time Activity

7:00 AM Wake up

8:00 PM Start bedtime routine

8:30 PM Bedtime

Introduce "Quiet Time"

If your child refuses to nap, it's best not to force it. Instead, institute "quiet time." This is a dedicated period where they stay in their room engaging in calm, quiet activities like looking at picture books, drawing, or listening to calm music . This allows their body to rest and can often lead to sleep naturally.

Adjust Bedtime

When dropping the last nap, an earlier bedtime is a helpful tool to prevent overtiredness. You may need to temporarily move their bedtime up by about 20minutes . A late afternoon nap that ends after 3:00 PM can push back their nighttime sleep.

Maintain a Consistent Bedtime Routine

A predictable wind-down routine is key to helping your child settle at night. This can include a warm bath, brushing teeth, and reading a story. This helps signal that it's time to transition from the busyness of the day to a calm state for sleep . Also, turn off all screens at least an hour before bed, as the blue light can interfere with sleep .

When to See a Doctor

Speak with your pediatrician if you are worried about your child's sleep, or if your child snores loudly, has unusual nighttime fears, or if sleep problems are affecting their daytime behavior .
