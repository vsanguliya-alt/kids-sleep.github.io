1-Year-Old Nap Schedule: A Guide for Parents

Navigating your 1-year-old's sleep can feel like a puzzle. By their first birthday, a child's sleep needs are changing, and establishing a consistent nap routine becomes key. One-year-olds need 11 to 14 hours of good quality sleep every 24 hours, including naps . This is often divided into 10hours at night and 1 to 2.5 hours of daytime sleep .

How Many Naps Does a 1-Year-Old Need?

Between 12 and 18 months, most toddlers transition from two naps to one . So, for a 1-year-old, the nap schedule can look slightly different depending on their individual readiness.

On a Two-Nap Schedule: If your child is on two naps, they will likely nap in the morning and early afternoon, with about 3.5 hours of awake time in between .

Transitioning to One Nap: By the time they are 12 to 15 months old, many children will naturally settle into a single, longer nap in the middle of the day .

How do you know when your child is ready to drop their morning nap? Look for signs like consistently fighting the morning nap, taking a very long time to fall asleep for a nap, or the nap causing them to stay up too late at night . Every child is different, and their sleep patterns will determine their needs .

Tips for a Successful Nap Routine

Creating a predictable routine can make nap time easier for everyone. Here are some proven tips to help your child get the rest they need :

Stick to a Schedule: Aim for naps at the same time each day to help regulate your child's inner clock. If you're following a two-nap schedule, 9 a.m. and 1 p.m. are good target times . For one nap, a midday time, such as 12:30 or 1 p.m., works well.

Set the Mood: Make sure the room is dark, quiet, and cool, as this helps signal that it's time to rest .

Wind Down: A short calming routine before a nap—reading a story or singing a soft lullaby—can help your child transition .

Put Your Child Down Drowsy but Awake: Placing them in their crib while they are sleepy but still awake teaches them to fall asleep independently .

What if Your Child Fights a Nap?

It's common for children to resist a nap occasionally. This is often a sign that a schedule change might be needed . If your child is fighting their second nap, it could be time to transition to one longer afternoon nap .

If you have questions or worries about your baby's napping, don't hesitate to talk to your baby's healthcare professional .
