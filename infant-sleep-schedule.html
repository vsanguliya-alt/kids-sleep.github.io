Infant Sleep Schedule: A Guide for Parents

Creating a consistent sleep schedule is one of the most effective ways to ensure your baby gets the rest they need for healthy growth and development. While every baby is unique, understanding age-appropriate sleep patterns can help you establish a routine that works for your family.

Why Sleep Schedules Matter

Quality sleep is essential for your baby&#x27;s brain development, learning, memory, and overall health. It&#x27;s not just the number of hours of sleep that matters—the quality of your sleep is also important.

Infant Sleep Schedule by Age

As your baby grows, their sleep needs change significantly. Here is a general guide to what you can expect at each stage.

0 to 3 Months

Newborns have no established day-night rhythm yet. They sleep in short bursts of 1 to 4 hours and are awake for 1 to 2 hours at a time. During the first month, babies spend about 16 hours a day sleeping, with sleep periods spaced evenly between feedings. After being awake for 1 to 2 hours, they most often need to sleep again.

4 to 6 Months

Between 4 and 6 months, many babies can begin to adopt a more regular day-night schedule. Most babies this age still need 2 to 3 naps per day. A typical pattern is a mid-morning nap and an early afternoon nap, with some babies also needing a late-afternoon catnap.

6 to 12 Months

At this age, most babies are on a consistent two-nap schedule—one in the morning and one in the afternoon. Babies 10 to 12 months old often drop the morning nap, and by 9 months, it is common to phase out the late-afternoon nap to help your baby go to bed earlier at night. Babies over 9 months of age should not sleep after 3:30 PM, as it can lead to a later bedtime.

12 to 18 Months

After their first birthday, most children transition to one nap per day, typically in the middle of the day. By about 18 months, many toddlers are on a single afternoon nap lasting 1 to 2 hours.

Safe Sleep Essentials

Room Temperature: Keep the room at a comfortable temperature for an adult.

Safe Sleep: Always place your baby on their back to sleep on a firm, flat mattress. Keep the sleep area clear of soft objects, pillows, and loose bedding.

Room-Sharing: Have your baby sleep in your room for at least the first six months, but on a separate surface designed for infants.

Tips for a Successful Schedule

Watch for tired cues: Yawning, eye-rubbing, and fussiness are signs it&#x27;s time for a nap.

Create a consistent routine: A short, calming pre-nap or pre-bed routine helps signal that it&#x27;s time to sleep.

Put your baby down drowsy but awake: This helps them learn to self-soothe.

Be flexible: If your baby is unwell or going through a developmental leap, their sleep needs may vary.

Trust your instincts. If you are worried about your baby&#x27;s sleep, call your health care provider right away.
