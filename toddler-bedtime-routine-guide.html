Toddler Bedtime Routine: Complete Parent Guide

A consistent, calming bedtime routine is one of the most powerful tools for helping your toddler—and your family—get the restful sleep everyone needs. For children aged 1 to 3, the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends 11 to 14 hours of good quality sleep every 24 hours, including naps and consistent sleep and wake-up times . This guide covers what pediatricians recommend for building a successful routine, troubleshooting common challenges, and knowing when to seek help.

The Gold Standard: Brush, Book, Bed

The AAP’s simple, evidence-based framework—Brush, Book, Bed—is designed to help families build a predictable nighttime ritual that supports oral health, early literacy, and healthy sleep .

Step 1: Brush

For children under 3 years: As soon as you see a tooth, brush with a smear (grain of rice) of fluoride toothpaste twice per day .

For children ages 3–6: Use a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste twice per day. Let your child practice, but you should still handle the brushing to ensure a thorough job .

The key rule: The last thing to touch your child's teeth before bed is the toothbrush—not a bottle, cup, or snack . Avoid putting babies to bed with a bottle of milk, formula, or juice.

Step 2: Book

Reading together for just 15 minutes each night builds vocabulary, supports school readiness, and creates calm, connected moments at the end of the day .

Use silly voices, make up stories, and let your child choose a favorite book to give them a sense of control.

Step 3: Bed

Set a regular bedtime and stick to it—even on weekends. A consistent schedule helps children fall asleep faster, stay asleep longer, and wake up feeling rested .

Don’t wait for your child to start rubbing their eyes or yawning—that’s often too late. Putting your child to bed even 15 to 20 minutes earlier can make a big difference .

Building a Calming Routine

The hour before bed should be quiet and soothing, not active and exciting . Here’s a sample routine you can adapt:

Time Activity

30 minutes before bed Turn off screens, dim the lights, put toys away

20 minutes before bed Warm bath, put on pajamas, brush teeth

10 minutes before bed Read a book (or two!), sing a quiet song, give hugs, say goodnight

Key tips:

Be consistent: The same routine, the same time, every night .

Avoid screens: Turn off TVs, tablets, and phones at least 30–60 minutes before bed .

Keep the room cool, dark, and quiet: A small night light is okay if your child is afraid of the dark .

Offer a comfort object: A safe stuffed animal or special blanket can help children feel secure, especially if they wake during the night .

Common Challenges and Solutions

Bedtime Resistance and Delaying Tactics

If your toddler calls out for "one more" drink, book, or hug, consistency is key. Setting firm boundaries helps develop proper sleep habits . If they come out of their room, walk or carry them back calmly without engaging in play or conversation .

Night Wakings and Wandering

Many toddlers wake at night and wander to their parents' room. The best approach is to calmly walk or carry them back to their own bed with minimal fuss and attention . Children should not be punished for this, as they may not be fully awake.

Transitioning to a "Big Bed"

Wait until at least age 3 unless your toddler is climbing out of the crib regularly .

During the transition, reassure your toddler and make sure the sleeping environment is safe .

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your pediatrician if your toddler:

Snores loudly or has trouble breathing during sleep Is excessively tired during the day Has significant sleep problems that last more than a few weeks, despite consistent routines The Bottom Line

A predictable bedtime routine is essential for a toddler's health, learning, and mood . By following the simple "Brush, Book, Bed" framework and staying consistent, you can help your child develop healthy sleep habits that will benefit them—and your family—for years to come.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my toddler's sleep?

Call your pediatrician if your child snores loudly, has trouble breathing during sleep, is excessively tired during the day, or if sleep problems persist for more than a few weeks despite consistent routines .

Q: How long does a normal bedtime routine take?

A typical bedtime routine takes about 20 to 30 minutes. The exact time will vary based on your child's age and the specific steps you include .

Q: What home remedies help with bedtime resistance?

Establish a consistent, calm routine and avoid screens for an hour before bed. Offer a comfort object like a safe stuffed animal, and ensure your child's needs (water, bathroom, a night light) are met before lights out .

Q: Should I let my toddler sleep with a stuffed animal?

Yes, it's safe and can be comforting for a toddler to sleep with a favorite stuffed animal, blanket, or toy. Make sure it's free of choking hazards like buttons or loose ribbons .

Q: Is it normal for my toddler to fight bedtime?

Yes, toddlers often resist bedtime as they test boundaries and assert independence . A consistent, predictable routine is the best way to address this.

Q: When should I move my toddler from a crib to a big bed?

Wait until at least age 3 unless your child is regularly climbing out of the crib. Moving too early can create new sleep challenges .

Q: How important is a consistent bedtime?

Very important. A consistent bedtime helps set your child's internal body clock, making it easier for them to fall asleep at the same time each night and wake up feeling rested .
