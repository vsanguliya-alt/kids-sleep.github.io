Understanding the 18-Month Sleep Regression

What Is the 18-Month Sleep Regression?

The 18-month sleep regression is a common phase where a toddler who previously slept well suddenly starts waking at night, resisting naps, or fighting bedtime. This is a normal developmental stage, not a sign that you&#x27;ve done something wrong. It is exciting to watch your toddler learn new skills (Source: NIH | Toddler Development), and this period of rapid development can affect their sleep.

Why Does It Happen?

At around 18 months, toddlers experience major developmental leaps. They are learning new words, walking more confidently, and developing a stronger sense of independence. These exciting changes can make it harder for them to &quot;power down&quot; at night. This phase often coincides with developmental milestones for 18 months (1.5 years) (Source: NIH | Toddler Development).

How Long Does It Last?

The 18-month sleep regression typically lasts 2 to 6 weeks, though it varies from child to child. While it can be frustrating, it is a temporary phase that usually resolves on its own with consistency.

How to Manage the Regression

Stick to a consistent routine. Children thrive on predictability. A regular bedtime routine helps signal that it&#x27;s time to sleep. Well-child visits are important to your toddler&#x27;s health, and your pediatrician can offer guidance on sleep routines (Source: NIH | Toddler Health).

Keep calm and be reassuring. If your toddler wakes at night, respond calmly and quickly. A quick cuddle or some gentle words can help them feel secure without creating new sleep habits.

Maintain age-appropriate boundaries. It&#x27;s okay to offer comfort, but try to avoid bringing your toddler into your bed unless that is a long-term plan for your family.

Ensure your toddler gets enough nutrients each day as part of their overall health (Source: NIH | Toddler Nutrition).

When to Call the Doctor

If you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH | Toddler Health). You should also contact your pediatrician if the sleep disturbances last more than 2 to 3 weeks, are accompanied by other concerning symptoms (like fever, signs of illness, or extreme fussiness), or if you are worried about your child&#x27;s development (Source: NIH | Toddler Development).
