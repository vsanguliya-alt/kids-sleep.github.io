Baby Won't Sleep Alone? A Guide for Parents

It is very common for a baby to resist sleeping alone. This phase is often tied to normal developmental stages, such as separation anxiety, a fear of being separated from a parent . This can lead to:

Refusing to Sleep Alone: The simple act of you leaving can cause real distress.

Frequent Night Wakings: Your baby may wake up and need reassurance that you are still nearby.

Bedtime Battles: Resistance to going to sleep.

How to Help Your Baby Sleep Alone

The key is to help your baby feel secure while gently encouraging independence. The sources provided offer a few key strategies, though more detailed guidance is limited.

Safe Sleep Environment is Essential

A crucial part of a baby's sleep is their environment. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the NIH provide clear safety guidelines to reduce the risk of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) :

Always place your baby on their back to sleep, even for short naps .

Use a firm, flat sleep surface like a crib mattress, covered with a fitted sheet .

Room-sharing is recommended for at least the first six months—your baby should sleep in a crib or bassinet close to you, but not in your bed .

Keep the crib clear of soft objects, pillows, and loose bedding .

Acknowledge Their Feelings

While the provided sources don't offer specific sleep training methods, general child development advice suggests validating a child's feelings can help them feel understood and secure.

When to Seek Help

If your baby's sleep difficulties are severe and last longer than a few weeks, or if you are worried about your baby's development, speak to your pediatrician. They can help rule out any underlying issues and offer personalized guidance.
