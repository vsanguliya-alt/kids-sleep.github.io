Newborn Snoring: When to Worry and What to Do

Hearing your newborn snore can be worrying. The good news is that for most babies, it&#x27;s perfectly normal and nothing to worry about. However, understanding the difference between harmless snoring and a potential problem can help you decide when to seek advice.

Why Newborns Snore

At a young age, a newborn&#x27;s airway is still very small and narrow. Snoring is often simply the sound of air passing through this tiny space. The most common, harmless reason for snoring is a blocked or stuffy nose from a cold or dry air. Other temporary causes include:

Position: Just like adults, newborns can snore more when sleeping on their backs.

Congestion: A common cold, allergies, or even irritants like dust can cause swelling and mucus.

Loud Snoring and Sleep-Disordered Breathing

While occasional, soft snoring is common, loud snorting, breathing, and snoring at night can sometimes be a sign of a condition called sleep-disordered breathing. This is when a child&#x27;s breathing is partially or completely blocked during sleep.

It&#x27;s important to look at the bigger picture. Snoring is often a symptom, not the problem itself. If your newborn snores loudly most nights, watch for these other signs:

Pauses in breathing followed by gasping or snorting.

Restless sleep and frequent tossing and turning.

Mouth breathing at night.

Poor weight gain or difficulty feeding.

Excessive daytime sleepiness. A newborn who snores loudly and is also very sleepy during the day may be experiencing poor-quality sleep at night.

When to See a Doctor

Occasional, quiet snoring that comes and goes with a cold is not a concern. However, you should contact your pediatrician if your newborn:

Snores loudly most nights of the week.

Has pauses in breathing or gasps for air during sleep.

Is a restless sleeper.

Has difficulty feeding or is not gaining weight well.

Seems unusually tired or irritable during the day.

If your newborn ever stops breathing, turns blue, or has a seizure, call emergency services immediately. While rare, any breathing difficulty in a newborn should be taken seriously.
