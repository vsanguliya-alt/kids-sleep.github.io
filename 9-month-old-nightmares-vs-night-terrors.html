9-Month-Old Nightmares vs. Night Terrors: 7 Signs Parents Should Know

It's terrifying to hear your 9-month-old suddenly scream in the middle of the night. This is a common experience that often points to either a nightmare or a night terror. While they may seem similar, these are two distinct sleep events. Understanding the 7 key signs can help you tell them apart and know how to respond.

What is a Nightmare?

A nightmare is a bad dream that occurs during REM (dreaming) sleep, which is more common in the second half of the night . A child who has a nightmare is usually fully or partially awake and will remember the dream or the feeling of fear. They often seek comfort and are usually easy to soothe .

What is a Night Terror?

A night terror is a parasomnia—a sleep disorder that happens during deep, non-REM sleep. They typically occur in the first few hours after falling asleep . During a night terror, the child appears extremely distressed but is actually asleep. They are difficult to rouse and will have no memory of the event the next morning . While they look alarming to parents, night terrors are generally considered harmless and normal for this age .

7 Signs to Tell Them Apart

Here are the key differences to look for.

Sign Nightmare Night Terror

1. Timing Often occurs in the second half of the night or early morning . Usually occurs 1 to 3 hours after falling asleep .

2. State of Child Child is fully or partially awake. They may be upset but can respond to your voice . Child is still asleep. They appear awake but will not recognize you or respond to comfort .

3. Memory May remember the dream or the feeling of being scared the next day . No memory of the event the next morning .

4. Response to Comfort Welcomes comfort. They will accept hugs and reassurance from a parent . Unresponsive or inconsolable. They may push you away or thrash, and attempts to comfort them can make it worse .

5. Ability to Wake Easy to wake up . Very difficult to wake up. Trying to wake them can lead to more confusion and distress .

6. Behavior May cry and look for a parent. They might be able to talk about the scary dream . May scream, kick, thrash, or sit up in bed with a terrified look, while their eyes may be open but they don't see you .

7. Episode Frequency Can have more than one per night . Usually only one episode per night .

What Should You Do?

Your response depends on which event you are witnessing:

For a Nightmare: Provide comfort. Reassure your child, offer a hug, and help them settle back to sleep .

For a Night Terror: Do not try to wake them. This can cause more distress and confuse them . Stay close to ensure they are safe and cannot hurt themselves. Let the episode pass (usually 5minutes), and they will often fall back to sleep peacefully .

Reducing Triggers

Overtiredness and irregular sleep schedules are known triggers for night terrors . Maintaining a consistent, calming bedtime routine and ensuring your baby gets enough daytime sleep can help reduce their frequency .

When to Call a Doctor

While both nightmares and night terrors are generally normal, you should talk to your pediatrician if:

Night terrors happen very frequently (several times a week) .

An episode lasts longer than 30 minutes .

During an episode, your child has rhythmic jerking, stiffening, or lip-smacking movements, which could indicate a seizure .

Your child is injuring themselves during an episode .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my 9-month-old's night waking?

Call your pediatrician if the episodes are frequent, lasting longer than 30 minutes, or if your child shows concerning movements like stiffening or rhythmic jerking . For other concerns, contact your pediatrician.

Q: How long do night terrors last in a 9-month-old?

A night terror episode typically lasts between 5 to 20 minutes, though they can occasionally last up to 30 minutes . For children who have them, they usually happen once or twice a month, and many outgrow them by the time they reach preschool age.

Q: What home remedies help with my baby's night terrors?

The best home remedy is prevention through a consistent sleep schedule. Ensuring your 9-month-old is not overtired can help reduce triggers. A calming bedtime routine with a bath, book, and soft music can also be beneficial .

Q: Can my 9-month-old be having nightmares this early?

Yes, babies can experience nightmares at this age . While they can't tell you about a bad dream, nightmares are common and often linked to developmental milestones or separation anxiety .

Q: Should I wake my baby during a night terror?

No, it is best not to try to wake them. Waking a child during a night terror can cause more confusion and distress. It is safer to stay nearby and let the episode pass .

Q: Does teething cause night terrors in babies?

Teething is not a direct cause of night terrors, but the discomfort can contribute to disrupted sleep. Overtiredness and being sick with a fever are more common triggers for night terrors .

Q: When should I go to the ER for my 9-month-old with a night terror?

Go to the ER immediately if your baby has trouble breathing, turns blue or grey, is difficult to wake, or has rhythmic jerking movements or stiffening. These are emergency signs.
