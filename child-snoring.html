Child Snoring: What Parents Need to Know

Snoring in children is a common concern. While it can be a normal occurrence, especially if a child has a cold or nasal congestion, persistent and loud snoring can sometimes signal an underlying health issue.

When Snoring Might Be a Concern

Snoring becomes a potential problem when it is loud and accompanied by other symptoms. The NHS lists loud snorting, breathing, and snoring at night as key signs that may be linked to excessive daytime sleepiness . This condition can make a child feel the need to sleep anywhere, sometimes without warning.

Loud snoring is often associated with a sleep-related breathing disorder, such as sleep apnea. This is a condition where a child's breathing is repeatedly blocked during sleep, causing them to wake up briefly to resume breathing. This disrupts the sleep cycle and prevents a child from getting deep, restful sleep.

Signs to Look For

If your child snores, look for these additional signs that may indicate a problem:

Excessive daytime sleepiness or fatigue: This could manifest as falling asleep during the day, difficulty waking up in the morning, or unusual tiredness.

Behavioral changes: This includes low mood, little interest in things, or mood swings that range from extreme highs to extreme lows .

Problems with concentration or memory: Snoring can lead to poor-quality sleep, which can make it difficult for a child to focus and perform well at school .

Breathing difficulties: Snoring accompanied by loud snorting or gasping for air during sleep is a red flag .

Pauses in breathing: This is a serious sign. While breath-holding can occur in young children and usually resolves by age 4, pauses in breathing during sleep require medical attention .

When to See a Doctor

If your child's snoring is persistent, loud, and accompanied by any of the signs above, you should schedule a visit with your pediatrician.

It's also important to consult a doctor if your child's snoring seems to be linked to:

Daytime sleepiness that affects their mood or ability to learn.

Problems recovering after physical activity .

Unusual feelings in their legs, particularly at night, which could be a sign of restless legs syndrome .

A doctor can evaluate your child's symptoms, check for other conditions, and, if necessary, refer you to a specialist for further assessment. This is especially important because snoring can sometimes be linked to other health problems that affect a child's performance at school, like problems with hearing or vision.
