Sleep Training a 9-Month-Old: Safe and Effective Methods

Sleep training can help babies learn to fall asleep independently and return to sleep on their own if they wake at night. At 9 months, babies are generally capable of sleeping through the night without feeding, but they may have developed habits that rely on parental help to fall asleep.

Evidence-Based Sleep Training Methods

Ferber Method (Graduated Extinction)

Put your baby to bed awake, leave the room, and return at progressively longer intervals to provide brief reassurance—for example, check at 3 minutes, then 5, then 10 minutes . The key is not to pick them up; offer verbal comfort only . This method balances structure with responsiveness . However, some babies become more upset with check-ins, so you may need to adjust .

The Chair Method (Gradual Withdrawal)

Sit in a chair next to your baby's crib until they fall asleep. Every few nights, move the chair farther away until you're outside the room . This allows you to be physically present while gradually encouraging independence . This approach may take longer but can be suitable for babies with separation anxiety or for parents with lower tolerance for crying .

Cry It Out Method (Full Extinction)

Place your baby in bed awake and do not return until morning—the fastest method, typically showing results in 3 to 5 days . This is one of the most empirically supported behavioral interventions . However, it can be emotionally challenging and requires ensuring all needs are met before bedtime .

Tips for Success

Create a consistent bedtime routine and a sleep environment that is dark, quiet, and comfortably cool . Consistency is the most important factor—if you are consistent, sleep training works quickly .
