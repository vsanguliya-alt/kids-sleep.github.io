Newborn Sleep Regression: What Parents Need to Know

A newborn&#x27;s sleep patterns can be unpredictable, and many parents wonder if their baby is going through a sleep regression. However, the concept of sleep regression is typically used to describe sleep disruptions in older babies and toddlers, not newborns.

Sleep Regressions in Older Babies

Sleep regressions are usually associated with specific developmental stages. They are typically defined by a sudden and often frustrating increase in night waking, shorter or skipped naps, and difficulty settling . These regressions are linked to major developmental leaps, such as learning to roll, sit, crawl, or talk .

How Sleep Changes in a Newborn

Newborn sleep is naturally irregular and does not follow the predictable patterns of older babies. It&#x27;s important to understand that what may seem like a &quot;regression&quot; in a newborn is often just a normal part of their development.

Newborns have a sleep cycle that is much shorter and more active (REM sleep) than older children. They need to feed frequently and are not yet able to self-soothe. This means that frequent night wakings are a normal part of newborn life.

What to Expect from a Newborn&#x27;s Sleep

Frequent wakings – newborns wake often to feed.

Short sleep periods – newborn sleep typically lasts 1 to 4 hours at a time.

No day-night pattern – newborns haven&#x27;t yet learned the difference between day and night.

Irregular sleep cycles – their sleep is still developing and is naturally inconsistent.

When to See a Doctor

If you are worried about your newborn&#x27;s sleep patterns or behavior, you should contact your healthcare provider. It is important to know the warning signs for more serious problems.

Trust your instincts. If you are worried about your newborn, call your health care provider right away.
