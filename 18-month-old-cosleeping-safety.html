Co-Sleeping Safety for Your 18-Month-Old

The safety of co-sleeping changes as your child grows. While major medical organizations strongly advise against bed-sharing during the first year of life, the conversation shifts after your child’s first birthday.

The First Year: A Strict &quot;No&quot;

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) clearly states that bed-sharing with infants under 1 year old is not recommended. Sleeping on the same surface as a baby increases the risk of entrapment, suffocation, and Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) . Babies in this age group may not be able to move their heads or bodies away from heavy bedding or a parent who might roll onto them . The AAP does recommend room-sharing —keeping your baby’s crib or bassinet in your room, next to your bed—for at least the first six months, as this practice can reduce the risk of SIDS by as much as 50% .

The Transition at 18 Months

Once your child reaches 18 months, the risk profile changes significantly. At this age, toddlers are more mobile, can roll over easily, and can typically alert or wake their parents if they are uncomfortable or in distress . While the AAP does not provide specific sleep guidelines for toddlers, many experts consider co-sleeping after the age of 1 to be generally safer than during infancy .

However, this doesn&#x27;t mean it is entirely risk-free.

Safety Considerations for an 18-Month-Old

Even at 18 months, safety precautions remain essential if you choose to bed-share.

Continue to Avoid: Adult beds still pose risks like falls, entrapment between the bed and wall, and suffocation from soft bedding. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) even recommends that children under the age of 2 not be placed in adult beds to sleep .

If You Choose to Bed-Share with a Toddler:

Use a firm mattress: Avoid soft surfaces where a child could sink in.

Remove pillows and heavy blankets: Minimize suffocation risks.

Secure the sleeping area: Place the mattress on the floor to prevent falls and ensure the bed is away from walls to avoid entrapment. The safest arrangement for an 18-month-old is a floor bed in a fully child-proofed room .

Ensure no other adults are impaired: No one in the bed should be under the influence of alcohol, drugs, or medications that cause heavy sleep .

Potential Downsides of Co-Sleeping

Beyond physical safety, experts note other factors to consider. Co-sleeping can make it harder for a toddler to learn to self-soothe and fall asleep independently, a critical skill for their development . Additionally, sharing a bed can lead to fragmented sleep for both parent and child.

The Safer Alternative

The safest approach for an 18-month-old is to transition them to their own safe sleep space. Families generally move toddlers from a crib to a bed between 18 months and 3 years, often when the child begins to climb out . A consistent bedtime routine and a comfortable &quot;big kid bed&quot; in the parent&#x27;s room or their own room can help ease this transition.

Ultimately, the decision to co-sleep is a personal one, but it should be made with a full understanding of the risks and benefits, prioritizing your child&#x27;s safety above all else.
