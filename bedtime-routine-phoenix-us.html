A Calm Bedtime Routine for Your Child: A Guide for Phoenix Parents

A predictable, soothing bedtime routine is one of the most powerful tools for helping your child—and you—get a good night's rest. For toddlers, a consistent routine not only signals that it's time to sleep but also provides a sense of security and helps prevent common bedtime struggles . The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) notes that bedtime problems are some of the most common issues seen in young children, and a consistent bedtime routine can help .

How Much Sleep Does a Toddler Need?

Before building a routine, it's helpful to know your child's sleep needs. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the AAP recommend that children aged 1 to 2 years get 11 to 14 hours of sleep per day, including naps . By around 18 months, most toddlers have transitioned to one nap per day, which should not be too close to bedtime .

Building the Perfect Bedtime Routine

The key to a successful routine is consistency and calmness. The AAP's "Brush, Book, Bed" program offers a simple, memorable framework :

1. Brush: A key part of the routine is oral care. Help your child brush their teeth before bed to prevent cavities and establish healthy habits . The last thing to touch their teeth should be the toothbrush—not a bottle or snack.

2. Book: Reading a story together is more than just a way to wind down; it builds vocabulary, strengthens your bond, and supports healthy brain development . Even 15 minutes of reading each night can make a big difference.

3. Bed: A regular, predictable bedtime helps your child fall asleep faster and stay asleep longer.

A good routine can also include a warm bath, putting on pajamas, and a gentle cuddle . Try to keep the sequence of activities the same each night and aim for the routine to start about 30 to 60 minutes before lights-out. This gives your child time to wind down from the day.

Creating a Sleep-Inducing Environment

A child's bedroom environment plays a significant role in their sleep quality. For younger toddlers, a transitional object like a favorite stuffed animal or blanket can help them feel safe and secure in their bed. This is particularly helpful if your child is going through a period of separation anxiety.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor about my child's sleep?

Contact your pediatrician if your child has persistent trouble falling asleep, snores loudly, or has gasping or pauses in breathing during sleep. Also, if you are worried about your child's sleep or behavior, talk to your child's health care provider.

How long does it take for a new bedtime routine to work?

It can take a few weeks for a new routine to become a habit. Consistency is the most important factor. If your child resists, stay calm and gently guide them back to bed.

What home remedies can help a child sleep?

A consistent, calming routine is the most effective "remedy." Avoid large meals and sugary snacks close to bedtime. Daily physical activity also supports good sleep.

What should I do if my child keeps getting out of bed?

Calmly and quietly return them to bed with minimal interaction. You can say, "It's time to sleep," and then leave. Avoid lectures or engaging in conversation.

What if my child is scared of the dark?

Acknowledge their fear and reassure them they are safe. Use a dim nightlight with a warm (red or orange) glow, which is less disruptive to sleep than blue light.
