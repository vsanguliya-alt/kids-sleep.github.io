Room Temperature for Child's Sleep: A Guide for Parents

Understanding the Importance of Room Temperature

Room temperature plays a significant role in a child's sleep quality and safety. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the NIH recommend keeping your baby's room at a temperature that is "comfortable for an adult" . This is to prevent overheating, which is a risk factor for Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) .

The Ideal Temperature Range

The advice to "keep the room at a comfortable temperature for an adult" is the core guideline for your child's sleep environment. The AAP suggests that if the room is too warm for you, it is likely too warm for your baby . A general temperature range of 68–72°F (20–22°C) is often recommended for a comfortable and safe sleep environment.

It is also important to check your child for signs of being too hot, such as sweating or feeling their chest is hot to the touch . Overheating can be a concern, especially for infants, but ensuring a comfortable room temperature is a key part of healthy sleep habits for children of all ages .

Safe Sleep Environment

In addition to temperature, a safe sleep environment is crucial. For babies, the following guidelines are recommended to reduce the risk of SIDS and sleep-related deaths:

Place your baby on their back to sleep, for naps and at night .

Use a firm, flat sleep surface, such as a crib mattress .

Keep soft objects and loose bedding out of the sleep area .

Room-sharing is recommended, but avoid bed-sharing .

Practical Tips for Managing Room Temperature

Use a room thermometer to monitor the temperature .

Dress your child in appropriate sleepwear. In warmer months, a short-sleeved onesie and sleep sack may be sufficient; in cooler months, a footed pajama or a sleep sack with long sleeves can be used .

Avoid heavy blankets, especially for infants under one year old .

If your child has a sleep problem or change in behavior, or if you have concerns about weight gain or loss, it's important to discuss these with your pediatrician .
