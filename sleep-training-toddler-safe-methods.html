Sleep Training a Toddler: Safe and Effective Methods

Teaching a toddler to sleep independently is a key step in their development, promoting better health, mood, and learning for the whole family. While there's no one-size-fits-all approach, these evidence-based methods focus on safety, consistency, and positive habits. Research suggests the key is helping toddlers learn to soothe themselves to sleep rather than relying on external aids .

Safe and Effective Methods

The goal is to help your toddler fall asleep alone and stay asleep. Here’s how:

1. Establish a Consistent Bedtime Routine

A predictable routine is the foundation of good sleep.

Same time every night: A consistent bedtime helps regulate your child's internal clock . Aim for a time that allows for 11hours of sleep for 1year olds, and 10hours for 3year olds, including naps .

Calming, predictable activities: A routine of bath, brushing teeth, and reading stories, all done in the same order, signals that sleep is coming .

Keep it brief and consistent: Avoid stimulating activities like active play or screen time, which includes tablets or audio stories that emit blue light . Reading a book is best .

2. The "Drowsy but Awake" Principle

Put your child to bed when they are drowsy but still awake. This teaches them to fall asleep on their own, so when they naturally wake at night, they can self-soothe back to sleep without your help . This habit is best started early .

3. The "Check and Console" Method

This method balances reassurance with encouraging self-soothing .

Set the stage: Ensure all your child's needs are met (a drink of water, a potty trip) before lights out .

Offer brief reassurance: Put your child to bed and leave the room. If they call out, wait a few minutes before briefly checking on them with a quiet word or a rub on the back, without picking them up .

Avoid "checking" as a reward: Returning to the room repeatedly can teach a child that calling out gets your attention, making the problem worse. If you're sure they are safe and well, try not to reappear every time they call out .

4. Comfort Objects and Security

Allowing a toddler to sleep with a small, safe comfort object can provide security and help them self-soothe . A teddy bear, special blanket, or favorite toy is often helpful, especially if they wake during the night. Ensure the object has no choking hazards .

Important Safety and Final Tips

Avoid melatonin: Sleep aids like melatonin are generally not recommended for toddlers. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine advises parents to consult a health care provider before giving it to children, as it's an unregulated supplement with potential risks .

No co-sleeping out of desperation: While cultural practices vary, co-sleeping as a quick fix can prolong sleep issues. It's better to use behavioral strategies to help your child learn to sleep independently .

Stay calm and consistent: Helping a toddler develop good sleep habits takes time and can be frustrating. A negative response from a parent can worsen sleep problems . Praise your child for learning to fall asleep alone . If sleep issues persist, talk to your child's healthcare professional .
