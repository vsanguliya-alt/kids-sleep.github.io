Sleep Training a Toddler: Safe and Effective Methods

Understanding Sleep Training

Sleep training helps children learn to fall asleep independently and return to sleep on their own if they wake at night. A consistent bedtime routine and a sleep-friendly environment are essential foundations for any method.

Evidence-Based Methods

Ferber Method (Graduated Extinction)

Put your toddler to bed awake, leave the room, and return at progressively longer intervals to provide brief reassurance—for example, check at 3 minutes, then 5, then 10 minutes . The key is not to pick them up; offer verbal comfort only . This is one of the most commonly implemented approaches, balancing structure with responsiveness . However, some toddlers become more upset with check-ins, so you may need to adjust . Evidence shows this method achieves similar efficacy outcomes to full extinction .

The Chair Method (Gradual Withdrawal)

Sit in a chair next to your toddler's crib until they fall asleep. Every few nights, move the chair farther away until you're outside the room . This allows you to be physically present while gradually encouraging independence . This approach may take longer but can be suitable for children with separation anxiety or for parents with lower tolerance for crying .

Cry It Out Method (Full Extinction)

Place your toddler in bed awake and do not return until morning—the fastest method, typically showing results in 3 to 5 days . This is one of the most empirically supported behavioral interventions . However, it can be emotionally challenging and requires ensuring all needs are met before bedtime .

Bedtime Fading

Shift your toddler's bedtime to better match their natural rhythm. For example, if your child cries for 30 minutes after a 7 PM bedtime, their natural bedtime may be closer to 7:30 PM. Move bedtime by 15 minutes each night until reaching the desired time .

Tips for Success

Create a consistent bedtime routine and a sleep environment that is dark, quiet, and comfortably cool . Consistency is the most important factor—if you are consistent, sleep training works quickly .
