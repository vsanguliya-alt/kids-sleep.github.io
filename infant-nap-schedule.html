Infant Nap Schedule: A Guide by Age

A consistent nap schedule is one of the most effective ways to ensure your infant gets the rest they need for healthy growth and development. While every baby is unique, understanding age-appropriate sleep patterns can help you establish a routine that works for your family.

Why Naps Matter

Naps are not just for parents to have a break—they play a crucial role in your baby's cognitive development, emotional regulation, and physical growth. Well-rested infants nap more consistently, and a good daytime sleep routine supports learning and memory consolidation.

Infant Nap Schedule by Age

As your baby grows, their sleep needs change significantly. Here is a general guide to what you can expect at each stage.

0 to 3 Months

Newborns have no established day-night rhythm yet. They sleep in short bursts of 1 to 4 hours and are awake for 1 to 2 hours at a time. During the first month, babies spend about 16 hours a day sleeping, with naps lasting 3 to 4 hours spaced evenly between feedings. After being awake for 1 to 2 hours, they most often need to sleep again.

4 to 6 Months

Between 4 and 6 months, many babies can begin to adopt a more regular day-night schedule. Most babies this age still need 2 to 3 naps per day. A typical pattern is a mid-morning nap and an early afternoon nap, with some babies also needing a late-afternoon catnap. This is also when babies often start to self-soothe, as the startle reflex begins to fade.

6 to 12 Months

At this age, most babies are on a consistent two-nap schedule—one in the morning and one in the afternoon. Babies 10 to 12 months old often drop the morning nap, and by 9 months, it is common to phase out the late-afternoon nap to help your baby go to bed earlier at night. Babies over 9 months of age should not sleep after 3:30 PM, as it can lead to a later bedtime.

12 to 18 Months

After their first birthday, most children transition to one nap per day, typically in the middle of the day. By about 18 months, many toddlers are on a single afternoon nap lasting 1 to 2 hours.

18 Months to 3 Years

Children usually continue to nap until around age 3. At 2 years old, a child typically needs about 11 to 14 hours of sleep in a 24-hour period, with a nap of 1 to 3 hours. If your child is sleeping well and seems content with a different schedule, it is likely fine for them.

Tips for Successful Naps

Set the mood. A dark, quiet room can help babies sleep.

Put babies to bed drowsy but awake. This helps them learn to fall asleep independently.

Stick to a consistent schedule. Babies get the most out of naps when they happen at the same time each day.

Look for sleep cues. Yawning, eye-rubbing, and fussiness are signs your baby is ready to sleep.

Keep the room cool. A comfortable temperature (around 68°F) promotes better sleep.

Trust your instincts. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, call your health care provider right away.
