18-Month-Old Night Waking: Understanding Causes and Solutions

Night waking is common in 18-month-olds and can be disruptive, but it is often a normal part of development. At this age, toddlers experience significant growth in language, motor skills, and social awareness, which can disturb sleep. Separation anxiety also peaks around this time, causing children to wake and seek reassurance that a parent is still there.

Common Causes of Night Waking

Several factors may be contributing to your child&#x27;s night waking, including:

Teething pain from molars.

Ear infections or other illnesses that cause discomfort.

Bedtime routine issues, like going to sleep too late or being overstimulated before bed.

Daytime changes, such as dropping a nap or learning new skills.

What to Do About Night Waking

Key strategies for managing night waking include:

Ensure your child can self-soothe. This is a crucial skill for sleeping through the night. If your toddler relies on you to fall asleep (like rocking or nursing), they will likely wake and need that same help to get back to sleep. Try putting your child down when they are drowsy but still awake.

Keep nighttime interactions brief and boring. If they wake, check on them briefly to ensure they are safe and comfortable, but avoid turning on bright lights, playing, or feeding them (unless they are sick). This helps reinforce that nighttime is for sleeping.

Stick to a consistent bedtime routine. A predictable, calming sequence of events (bath, book, song) signals that sleep is coming.

When to See a Doctor

Contact your pediatrician if night waking is accompanied by:

Loud snoring or pauses in breathing.

Night terrors or sleepwalking that occurs regularly.

Signs of illness such as fever, ear pain, or persistent cough.

Extreme daytime sleepiness that affects your child&#x27;s behavior.
