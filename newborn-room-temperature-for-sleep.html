Newborn Room Temperature for Sleep: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Maintaining the correct room temperature is a key factor in creating a safe sleep environment for your newborn, as overheating is a known risk factor for Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) .

The Recommended Temperature

Health organizations, including the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP), recommend that the ideal room temperature for a sleeping baby is comfortable for an adult . This is often interpreted as between 16°C and 20°C (62°F) . A temperature around 18°C is often cited as being &quot;just right.&quot;

Why This Temperature is Important

Reduces SIDS Risk: The chance of SIDS is higher in babies who get too hot . Overheating is a significant risk factor, and research indicates that babies are safer when they are a little bit cooler rather than too warm .

Promotes Comfort: A room within this range helps your baby sleep more soundly and reduces the chance of night waking due to discomfort .

How to Monitor and Manage the Temperature

Use a Room Thermometer: Keep a thermometer in your baby&#x27;s room to easily monitor the temperature .

Check for Signs of Overheating: Feel your baby&#x27;s tummy or the back of their neck. It should feel warm, but not hot or sweaty . Other signs include flushed or red cheeks and damp hair . A baby&#x27;s hands and feet may naturally feel cool, which is not a reliable indicator of their core temperature .

Dressing Your Baby for Sleep

Choosing the right sleepwear is the most effective way to manage your baby&#x27;s temperature.

Use Sleep Sacks: Well-fitting, sleeveless baby sleeping bags are a safe alternative to loose blankets . They come in different TOG (Thermal Overall Grade) ratings, which measure insulation .

A 2.5 TOG sleep sack is typically recommended for room temperatures between 16°C .

Layer Clothing: As a general guide for a 16°C room, dress your baby in a long-sleeved bodysuit or vest and a pair of long-sleeved pajamas underneath their sleep sack .

Avoid Hats: Never put a hat on your baby while they are sleeping indoors, as this can cause them to overheat. Babies lose excess heat through their heads, and covering it can prevent this .

Always place your baby on their back to sleep and keep soft objects, pillows, and loose bedding out of the cot to ensure a safe sleep environment.
