Screen Time Before Bed for Your Newborn: What You Need to Know

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends no screen time for babies under 18 months, except for video chatting with relatives . Even "background" TV, where a screen is on in the room, can be distracting and negatively affect parent-child interactions and the quality of play . For babies under 2, screen use is discouraged entirely.

How Screen Time Disrupts Sleep

The blue light emitted by screens interferes with the production of melatonin, the hormone that regulates sleep-wake cycles. This makes it harder for your baby to fall asleep and stay asleep. For a newborn, whose sleep cycles are still developing, this disruption can be even more significant.

Why This Matters for a Newborn

Critical brain development – at this age, your baby's brain is developing rapidly, and they learn best through real-world interactions.

Parent-child bonding – face-to-face time with you is essential for healthy development.

Sleep associations – if your baby associates screens with falling asleep, they may need that same stimulation to get back to sleep during natural night wakings.

How to Create a Screen-Free Wind-Down Routine

Instead of screen time, focus on establishing a calming bedtime routine that helps your baby settle. A consistent, predictable routine helps signal that it's time to sleep. A 20minute routine might include:

A warm bath

A gentle massage

Quiet cuddles

A lullaby or soft song

A short story (with a physical book, not a tablet)

Other Factors That Impact Sleep

Screen time is just one part of the sleep puzzle. It is also important to address other factors that contribute to a good night's sleep:

Room environment – keep the bedroom dark, cool (around 16°C), and quiet.

Safe sleep – place your baby on their back in a crib or bassinet with a firm mattress.

Remember: The safest place for your newborn to sleep is on their back in their own crib or bassinet, in your room for the first 6 months, with no soft objects, pillows, or loose bedding.
