1-Year-Old Sleep Schedule: A Parent's Guide

If your 1-year-old isn't sleeping well, it can be exhausting for the whole family. The good news is that by 12 months, most children are developmentally ready for a predictable sleep schedule. This guide outlines the sleep needs of a typical 1-year-old and provides evidence-based strategies to help them (and you) get a better night's rest.

How Much Sleep Does a 1-Year-Old Need?

A one-year-old needs 11 to 14 hours of good quality sleep over a 24-hour period, including naps . This total sleep time usually breaks down to about 10 to 12 hours at night and between 1 to 2.5 hours of daytime naps .

The Transition to One Nap

Most children will transition from two naps to one nap between the ages of 12 and 18 months . However, every child is different, and your child's sleep patterns will determine their specific needs . By about 10 months to 1 year, many babies drop their morning nap entirely . During this transition, you may need to shift the afternoon nap and bedtime earlier by about 30 minutes to help your child adjust . By age 3, most children will take a 1hour nap in the afternoon .

How to Build a Healthy Sleep Schedule

Establishing a consistent routine is key to helping your toddler sleep well.

1. Set a Consistent Bedtime and Wake Time

Children thrive on routine. Aim to have your child go to bed and wake up at roughly the same time every day, even on weekends . Their wake-up time should not vary by more than an hour between weekdays and weekends .

2. Prioritize a Soothing Bedtime Routine

A predictable wind-down routine signals to your child that it's time to sleep. Great Ormond Street Hospital recommends starting a routine 30 minutes to 2 hours before bedtime . This can include activities like:

A warm bath

Reading a story or listening to calm music

Brushing teeth and using the toilet Gentle cuddles and conversation before you leave the room 3. Put Them to Bed Drowsy but Awake

It's important for your child to learn how to fall asleep on their own . Put them to bed when they are tired but still awake . If they learn to self-soothe at bedtime, they will be more likely to settle themselves back to sleep if they wake up during the night .

4. Create a Sleep-Inducing Environment

Your child's bedroom environment plays a significant role in their sleep quality :

Darkness: Use blackout blinds to make the room dark at bedtime and naptime .

Temperature: Keep the room cool, ideally around 18°C (65°F) .

Quiet & Calm: The bedroom should be a place for sleep, not play. Remove toys and distractions .

No Screens: Avoid screens (TV, tablets, smartphones) in the bedroom, as the blue light disrupts sleep .

Night Waking and Wandering

It is common for toddlers to wake during the night. Do not punish your child for this, as they may not be fully awake . The best strategy is to calmly and quietly lead them back to their own bed with minimal fuss and attention, and settle them back to sleep .

When to See a Doctor

Talk to your pediatrician if your child’s sleep problems are persistent, causing them significant distress, or if you are worried about their health or development .
