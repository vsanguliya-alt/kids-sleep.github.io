Choosing a Sleep Training Method for Your Child

Sleep training is the process of teaching your child to fall asleep and stay asleep independently. There are several evidence-based approaches, and the best choice depends on your child&#x27;s age, temperament, and your family&#x27;s comfort level. For most children, behavioral interventions—including parent training and specific sleep strategies—have shown beneficial effects on night waking and overall sleep disturbance .

Methods for Babies and Toddlers

Ferber Method (Graduated Extinction)

This method involves putting your child to bed awake and checking on them at progressively longer intervals if they cry . You offer brief reassurance (a few soothing words, a gentle pat) without picking them up, then leave again . The intervals increase each night, teaching your child to self-soothe .

Best for: Babies 4months and older; works well for families who want a structured approach with check-ins.

Pros: Often produces results within a week ; more tolerable than full &quot;cry-it-out.&quot;

Cons: Involves some crying; may not suit families uncomfortable with this.

Chair Method

You sit in a chair next to your child&#x27;s crib until they fall asleep, then gradually move the chair farther away over several nights until you are out of the room .

Best for: Children with separation anxiety; older toddlers and preschoolers.

Pros: Gentle approach; provides comfort by keeping you present .

Cons: Requires significant patience and time; can be hard for parents to stay seated while child fusses .

Pick Up, Put Down Method

When your child cries, you pick them up to soothe them but put them back down while they are still awake—before they fall asleep . You repeat as needed until they drift off on their own .

Best for: Parents who want a more hands-on approach.

Pros: Provides direct physical comfort ; considered gentler by some.

Cons: Can take a long time and require multiple repetitions; may be exhausting for parents .

Gradual Retreat Approach

This gentle method involves sitting next to your child&#x27;s cot while they fall asleep, then gradually moving your chair farther away every few nights until you are no longer in the room . It helps your child get used to falling asleep without you present, and may take a few weeks to work .

Gentle Methods for Older Children (Preschoolers and Up)

For children aged 3 years and older, gentler techniques can be especially effective.

Excuse Me Drill

You leave the room for very short breaks multiple times during the interval between lights out and when your child falls asleep . You make a brief excuse (&quot;I need to check on something—I&#x27;ll be right back!&quot;) and return before your child gets stressed, offering calm praise . You progressively increase the length of time you spend out of the room each break. This technique works well for anxious children who need a parent to fall asleep .

Taking a Break

A simpler version where you leave once at the midpoint of the interval between when you turn off the lights and when your child falls asleep, gradually increasing how long you stay out . This requires less energy from tired parents.

Key Principles for Success

Consistency is crucial. A consistent bedtime routine—bath, book, cuddle—helps signal that sleep is approaching . Put your child to bed drowsy but awake so they learn to fall asleep independently. Create a sleep-friendly environment: dark room, comfortable temperature, and no screens before bed .

If your child is unwell or teething, pause sleep training and start again when they are better .
