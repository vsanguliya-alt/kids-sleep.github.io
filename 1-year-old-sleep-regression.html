Understanding the 1-Year-Old Sleep Regression

A sleep regression at 12 months is common, but it can feel very confusing for parents. Your baby, who may have been sleeping through the night, might suddenly start waking frequently, fighting naps, or waking very early in the morning. This is often a sign that your child is experiencing a developmental leap, and it's usually temporary.

Why Does a 12-Month Sleep Regression Happen?

The 12-month sleep regression is primarily linked to your baby's rapid development. Around this age, children are reaching major milestones. They may be learning to walk, talk, or becoming more independent. This mental and physical growth can disrupt sleep patterns as their brains are busy processing new skills. They may be so eager to practice their new abilities that they have trouble powering down for sleep.

Signs Your Child Is in a Regression

You might notice your 1-year-old doing some of these things:

Resisting naps or bedtime — they may become more defiant and fight the routine.

Waking up often at night and having trouble settling back to sleep.

Waking very early in the morning and being unable to go back to sleep.

Increased clinginess or separation anxiety during the day.

What You Can Do to Help

Although this phase can be frustrating, there are some effective ways to manage it.

Keep a consistent routine: Children thrive on predictability. Maintaining a regular and calming bedtime routine (like a warm bath, reading a book, and then lights out) is more important than ever during a regression.

Encourage self-soothing: If your child relies on you to fall asleep (like rocking or nursing), they will likely wake up and need that same help to get back to sleep. Try putting your child down to sleep when they are drowsy but still awake to help them learn to self-soothe.

Check for illness or discomfort: Sometimes, what looks like a regression could actually be a sign that your child isn't feeling well. Check for signs of teething, fever, or other infections. If you are concerned, contact your pediatrician.

Prioritize a safe sleep environment: Ensure your child's room is dark and quiet, and that they are sleeping comfortably. Keep soft objects and loose bedding out of the crib to ensure safe sleep. This is important for good quality sleep.

When Will It End?

The 12-month sleep regression usually lasts a few weeks. Consistency and patience are key. If the sleep disturbances persist or if you are worried about your child's health, check with your pediatrician to rule out any underlying issues.
