Bedtime Routine for a 2-Year-Old: A Parent's Guide

At 2 years old, your child is full of energy and rapidly developing new skills. A consistent bedtime routine is more important than ever to help them wind down and get the sleep they need for healthy growth and emotional regulation.

How Much Sleep Does a 2-Year-Old Need?

A 2-year-old generally needs about 11 to 14 hours of sleep in a 24-hour period . This usually consists of 10 to 11 hours of sleep at night and 1 to 2 hours of daytime sleep, typically in the form of a single afternoon nap .

Building a Successful Bedtime Routine

A predictable, calming routine signals to your child that it's time to relax and prepare for sleep. It can help ease separation anxiety and reduce bedtime resistance.

Before Bedtime

Turn off screens: Power down all televisions, tablets, and other screens at least an hour before bed, as screen time is stimulating and can disrupt sleep.

Avoid stimulating activity: Keep the environment calm. Avoid active play, running around, or large meals just before the routine begins.

The Routine Itself

Aim for a routine that lasts about 20 to 45 minutes. It should be a consistent sequence of events your child can learn and predict.

Calming Activities: A warm bath, a gentle massage, or quiet cuddles are excellent ways to relax your child.

Brush Teeth & Clean Nappy: Complete these essential tasks as part of the routine.

Connection Time: Read a short bedtime story, sing a lullaby, or chat quietly about the day. This is a crucial time for positive connection.

Offer Limited Choices: Give your child some control, like choosing which pajamas to wear or which book to read, but limit the options to avoid negotiation.

Drowsy but Awake: Put your child to bed when they are sleepy but still awake. This helps them learn to fall asleep on their own and resettle if they wake during the night.

The Sleep Environment

Dim the Lights: Use a yellow or red dim nightlight so they don’t feel upset if they wake up, but keep it out of their direct line of sight.

Comfort Object: A favorite soft toy or blanket can be a great source of comfort and security.

Create a Calm Space: Keep the room dark, quiet, and cool.

Dealing with Resistance

It is common for 2-year-olds to test boundaries at bedtime, asking for "one more story" or "one more drink" as a way to seek connection and practice their autonomy. Stay calm, be consistent, and stick to the limits you have set. Keeping your nighttime visits "brief and boring" can help.

Well-child visits are important to your toddler's health, and routine exams and screenings help you and your kids prevent and treat health problems as well as chart their growth and development . If you have concerns about your child's sleep, discuss them with your pediatrician.
