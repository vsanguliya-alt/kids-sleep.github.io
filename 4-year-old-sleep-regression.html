Understanding the 4-Year-Old Sleep Regression

A sleep regression at 4 years old is a common, temporary phase when a preschooler who previously slept well suddenly refuses to go to sleep or wakes up during the night . It often coincides with periods of high growth and development .

Signs Your Child Is in a Regression

You might notice your 4-year-old displaying one or more of the following signs:

Bedtime Battles: Increased resistance to going to bed, fussiness, or stalling at bedtime .

Night Wakings: Waking more frequently during the night and having difficulty settling back down .

Clinginess: More separation anxiety, crying out when caregivers leave the room, and needing extra reassurance .

Nap Disruptions: Refusing naps, taking shorter naps, or sleeping longer during the day .

Tantrums: Heightened agitation or irritability when it&#x27;s time to sleep .

What Causes It

At 4 years old, preschoolers are often experiencing a surge in independence, developing new communication skills, and navigating big emotions . Separation anxiety often intensifies during this stage, which can lead to more night wakings . The regression is usually temporary and often associated with high periods of growth and development .

How Long Does It Last?

While every child is different, the 4-year sleep regression typically lasts anywhere from 2 to 6 weeks . Consistency and patience are key during this challenging but temporary phase .
