Infant Refusing Naps? A Guide for Parents

It can be incredibly frustrating when your baby, who used to nap like a champion, suddenly starts refusing to sleep during the day. This is a common challenge for parents and is often a sign of a developmental shift or an unmet need, rather than a permanent change.

Common Reasons for Nap Refusal

1. Your Baby Is Ready for a Nap Transition

Babies&#x27; sleep needs change rapidly in the first two years. What worked a month ago may not work today. Common times for nap transitions include:

By 3–4 months: Down to 4 naps

By 5–6 months: Down to 3 naps

By 7–9 months: Down to 2 naps

By 14–18 months: Ready for just one nap per day

If your baby is in one of these windows, the resistance is likely a sign that their sleep schedule needs updating. For example, most babies drop their third nap around 9 months old.

2. Your Baby Is Overtired

This might seem counterintuitive, but an overtired baby can actually be harder to settle. When a baby gets too tired, stress hormones can make it difficult for them to calm down and fall asleep. Look for early signs of tiredness like eye-rubbing, yawning, or fussiness, and try to put them down for a nap before they become overtired.

3. Your Baby Is Not Tired Enough (Low Sleep Pressure)

Naps are largely driven by a biological process called &quot;sleep pressure,&quot; which builds up during awake periods. If your baby isn&#x27;t awake long enough before a nap, they simply won&#x27;t be tired enough to fall asleep. Adjusting their wake windows to be a little longer can help build that necessary sleep pressure.

4. The Sleep Environment Is Too Distracting

Once babies are out of the newborn stage, they are more aware of their surroundings and can be easily distracted by light or noise. A room that is too bright or noisy can make it difficult for them to settle. Consider using blackout curtains to darken the room and a white noise machine to muffle household sounds.

What You Can Do

Follow Your Baby&#x27;s Lead: Pay close attention to your baby&#x27;s sleep cues. If they are not showing signs of tiredness, don&#x27;t force a nap. Try again in 30 to 60 minutes.

Establish a Consistent Routine: Children thrive on predictability. Create a short, calming pre-nap routine—like reading a book or singing a lullaby—to help signal that sleep is coming. Put your baby down drowsy but awake.

Ensure a Safe Sleep Environment: Always place your baby on their back to sleep on a firm, flat surface. Keep soft objects, loose bedding, and toys out of the crib.

Be Patient: Nap resistance is often a phase. If you&#x27;ve tried everything and your baby still won&#x27;t nap, give them some quiet time in their crib for about 30 minutes to rest. Consistency is key.
