Bedtime Routine for a 3-Year-Old: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

A consistent bedtime routine is more important than ever for a 3-year-old. At this age, they are exploring their independence, which can lead to bedtime resistance and stalling. A predictable routine helps signal to your child that it&#x27;s time to wind down and get ready for sleep.

How Much Sleep Does a 3-Year-Old Need?

A 3-year-old generally needs about 10 to 13 hours of sleep in a 24-hour period . This typically includes a nap during the day, which often lasts 1 to 2 hours. However, many children this age may start to give up their nap altogether.

Building a Successful Bedtime Routine

A calming, consistent routine can help ease anxiety and reduce bedtime battles. Aim for a routine that lasts about 20 to 45 minutes.

Before Bedtime

Turn off screens: Power down all televisions, tablets, and other screens at least an hour before bed, as screen time is stimulating and can disrupt sleep.

Avoid stimulating activity: Keep the environment calm. Avoid active play, running around, or large meals just before the routine begins.

The Routine Itself

Calming Activities: A warm bath, gentle massage, or quiet cuddles are excellent ways to relax your child.

Brush Teeth &amp; Use the Toilet: Complete these essential tasks as part of the routine.

Connection Time: Read a short bedtime story, sing a lullaby, or chat quietly about the day. This is a crucial time for positive connection.

Offer Limited Choices: Give your child some control, like choosing which pajamas to wear or which book to read, but limit the options to avoid negotiation.

Drowsy but Awake: Put your child to bed when they are sleepy but still awake. This helps them learn to fall asleep on their own.

The Sleep Environment

Dim the Lights: Use a yellow or red dim nightlight so they don’t feel upset if they wake up, but keep it out of their direct line of sight.

Comfort Object: A favorite soft toy or blanket can be a great source of comfort and security.

Create a Calm Space: Keep the room dark, quiet, and cool.

Dealing with Resistance

It is common for 3-year-olds to test boundaries at bedtime, asking for &quot;one more story&quot; or &quot;one more drink&quot; as a way to seek connection and practice their autonomy. Stay calm, be consistent, and stick to the limits you have set. If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s sleep, discuss them with your pediatrician.
