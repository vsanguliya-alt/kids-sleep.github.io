Signs of Sleep Apnea in a 3-Year-Old

Sleep apnea is a common disorder that causes your breathing to stop or get very shallow during sleep (Source: NIH | Sleep Apnea). The most common type is obstructive sleep apnea, which occurs when your airway collapses or becomes blocked during sleep (Source: NIH | Sleep Apnea). While it is more common in adults, it can also affect young children, including 3-year-olds. Recognizing the signs early is important for your child's health.

Common Signs and Symptoms

For a 3-year-old, the signs of sleep apnea can be different from those in adults, but there are key symptoms to watch for.

Breathing Signs During Sleep:

Snoring: People with sleep apnea often snore loudly (Source: NIH | Sleep Apnea). If your child's snoring is persistent and loud, it could be a warning sign.

Pauses in Breathing: Your bed partner may notice that you stop breathing for short periods during sleep (Source: NIH | Sleep Disorders). This is a classic sign of sleep apnea.

Choking, Gasping, or Snorting: Your child might make choking sounds, snort, or gasp for air during sleep (Source: NIH | Sleep Disorders). This is often a sign that they are briefly waking up to resume breathing.

Daytime Signs:

Daytime Sleepiness: A child with sleep apnea may often feel sleepy during the day, take frequent naps, or fall asleep at the wrong times (Source: NIH | Sleep Disorders).

Poor Sleep Quality: If your child frequently has restless sleep, wakes up several times, or has trouble falling back to sleep, it could indicate a sleep disorder (Source: NIH | Sleep Disorders).

Risk Factors

Certain factors can increase the risk of sleep apnea in children. You are more at risk for sleep apnea if you are overweight, male, or have a family history or small airways (Source: NIH | Sleep Apnea). In young children, enlarged tonsils or adenoids are common causes of airway blockage.

When to Consult a Doctor

If you are worried about your child's sleep, it is essential to talk to your health care provider. You can discuss their medical and family history, and a physical exam can help identify any physical blockages (Source: NIH | Sleep Apnea). If sleep apnea is suspected, your doctor may recommend a sleep study to monitor your child's breathing, brain activity, and other vital signs during sleep (Source: NIH | Sleep Disorders).
