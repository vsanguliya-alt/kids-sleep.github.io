Understanding the 1-Year-Old Nap Transition

At around one year old, your child's sleep patterns are naturally evolving. Many toddlers begin to transition from two naps to one between 12 and 18 months of age . This is a significant change that affects their daytime energy and nighttime sleep.

How Much Sleep Does a 1-Year-Old Need?

A one-year-old typically needs about 11 to 14 hours of good quality sleep every 24 hours . By this age, most babies have dropped their morning nap and usually take one long nap in the afternoon . Until about age 3, most children still sleep for 1 to 2 hours in the afternoon , though every child is unique and their sleep patterns can vary.

When Do Toddlers Drop the Last Nap?

All children are different, but the transition to dropping the last nap often happens around 3 years old, though some may still nap until age 5 .

How to Handle the Transition

The key to managing this change is to watch for signs that your toddler is ready. Resistance to naps or difficulty falling asleep at night may indicate they no longer need daytime sleep . You can also consider offering a short nap or a quiet time instead . If your child is not cranky without their nap, it is a good sign they are ready.

When dropping a nap, it can help to temporarily move bedtime a bit earlier—about 20 to 30 minutes—to prevent your child from becoming overtired . Creating a consistent and calming routine, even for quiet time, can help them relax .
