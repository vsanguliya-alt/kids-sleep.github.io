Infant Snoring: When to Worry and What to Do

Hearing your baby snore can be worrying. The good news is that for most babies, it's perfectly normal and nothing to worry about. However, understanding the difference between harmless snoring and a potential problem can help you decide when to seek advice.

Why Babies Snore

At a young age, a baby's airway is still very small and narrow. Snoring is often simply the sound of air passing through this tiny space. The most common, harmless reason for snoring is a blocked or stuffy nose from a cold or dry air. Other temporary causes include:

Position: Just like adults, babies can snore more when sleeping on their backs.

Congestion: A common cold, allergies, or even irritants like dust can cause swelling and mucus.

Loud Snoring and Sleep-Disordered Breathing

While occasional, soft snoring is common, loud snorting, breathing, and snoring at night can sometimes be a sign of a condition called sleep-disordered breathing. This is when a child's breathing is partially or completely blocked during sleep.

It's important to look at the bigger picture. Snoring is often a symptom, not the problem itself. If your baby snores loudly most nights, watch for these other signs:

Pauses in breathing followed by gasping or snorting.

Restless sleep and frequent tossing and turning.

Mouth breathing at night.

Poor weight gain or difficulty feeding.

Excessive daytime sleepiness. A baby who snores loudly and is also very sleepy during the day may be experiencing poor-quality sleep at night.

When to See a Doctor

Occasional, quiet snoring that comes and goes with a cold is not a concern. However, you should contact your pediatrician if your baby:

Snores loudly most nights of the week.

Has pauses in breathing or gasps for air during sleep.

Is a restless sleeper.

Has difficulty feeding or is not gaining weight well.

Seems unusually tired or irritable during the day.

If your baby ever stops breathing, turns blue, or has a seizure, call emergency services immediately. While rare, any breathing difficulty in a baby should be taken seriously.
