Newborn Sleep Apnea Signs: What Parents Need to Know

Sleep apnea in newborns is a condition where your baby&#x27;s breathing pauses during sleep. While some breathing irregularities are normal in newborns, it&#x27;s important to recognize the signs that may indicate a more serious problem.

Normal Periodic Breathing vs. Sleep Apnea

Many newborns experience &quot;periodic breathing,&quot; which is a harmless and expected breathing pattern. This involves short pauses in breathing lasting 5 to 10 seconds, followed by a series of rapid breaths . This pattern typically occurs while your baby is sleeping and usually resolves on its own by 6 months of age .

Sleep apnea is different. It is diagnosed when pauses in breathing last 20 seconds or longer, or when a shorter pause is accompanied by a drop in heart rate or bluish skin color .

Key Signs of Sleep Apnea in Newborns

If your baby has sleep apnea, you may observe one or more of the following signs:

Breathing Signs

Pauses in breathing that last longer than 10 seconds .

Loud, uneven snoring or noisy, labored breathing during sleep .

Gasping, choking, or snorting sounds as your baby struggles to resume breathing .

Mouth breathing, even during the daytime .

Physical Signs

Changes in skin color – your baby&#x27;s skin, lips, or tongue may turn blue, pale, or gray. On darker skin, this may appear as yellow-gray or white .

Sweating or clamminess during sleep .

Difficulty feeding or poor weight gain (failure to thrive) .

Behavioral Signs

Frequent night waking, restlessness, or tossing and turning .

Excessive daytime sleepiness or difficulty waking .

Developmental delays – in severe cases, sleep apnea can affect growth and development .

Recurrent respiratory infections .

When to Call a Doctor or Seek Emergency Care

Trust your instincts. If your baby&#x27;s breathing doesn&#x27;t seem right, call a healthcare provider .

Seek emergency care immediately (call 911 or your local emergency number) if your baby :

Stops breathing for longer than 10 seconds.

Has trouble breathing – look for flaring nostrils, or the muscles between the ribs or in the belly pulling in with each breath (retractions).

Has skin that is blue, pale, or gray in color, especially around the mouth.

Is lethargic, not responding normally, or unconscious.

What Causes Sleep Apnea in Newborns?

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in infants is often related to craniofacial anomalies (differences in the structure of the face or skull) . In premature babies, apnea can occur because the parts of the body that control breathing are still developing (&quot;apnea of prematurity&quot;) . Other risk factors include low birth weight or a history of respiratory issues .
