Preschooler Sleep Schedule: A Guide for Parents

A consistent sleep schedule is one of the most important things you can do to support your child&#x27;s health and development. Quality sleep helps restore energy, supports learning and memory, and keeps your child healthy . For preschoolers, a predictable routine sets the stage for a good night&#x27;s rest.

How Much Sleep Does a Preschooler Need?

Preschoolers typically need about 10 to 13 hours of good quality sleep over a 24-hour period, which may be broken down into nighttime sleep and, for some, a daytime nap. At 3 to 4 years old, many children are transitioning away from their daytime nap, which affects their nighttime sleep needs.

The Components of a Healthy Sleep Schedule

A healthy sleep schedule is built on consistency and predictability.

Consistent Bedtime

A consistent bedtime is essential. Going to bed at roughly the same time each night (and getting up at the same time each morning) helps regulate your child&#x27;s internal clock, making it easier for them to fall asleep and wake up naturally. A typical bedtime for a preschooler is between 7:00 PM and 8:00 PM.

A Calming Bedtime Routine

A predictable, calming routine helps signal to your child that it&#x27;s time to transition from the busyness of the day to sleep. A 20minute wind-down period might include a bath, brushing teeth, and reading a story.

The Nap Dilemma

By preschool age, naps are often a thing of the past. Many 3- to 5-year-olds no longer need a daytime nap. If a child is still napping, it is important to ensure that the nap is short and ends by the early afternoon so it does not interfere with bedtime. A regular schedule that includes a consistent wake-up time and bedtime can help you decide if a nap is still needed.

Signs Your Child Needs a Schedule Adjustment

You may need to adjust your child&#x27;s schedule if they are:

Taking a long time to fall asleep at night.

Waking up frequently during the night.

Waking up very early in the morning.

Showing signs of being overtired or having difficulty concentrating during the day.

If sleep problems or changes in behavior persist, it&#x27;s worth checking in with your child&#x27;s healthcare provider.
