Bedtime Routine in Los Angeles: A Guide for Parents

Creating a consistent bedtime routine is one of the most effective ways to help your child—whether you're in Los Angeles or anywhere else—get the restful sleep they need for healthy growth and development. A predictable routine signals to a child's body that it's time to wind down, making it easier to fall asleep and stay asleep.

What Does a Good Bedtime Routine Look Like?

A healthy bedtime routine can start as early as 6 months of age and should be followed consistently. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends a simple, three-step process that families can easily remember: Brush, Book, Bed .

Brush: Each night, help your child brush their teeth before bed. The last thing that should touch their teeth is a toothbrush, not a bottle, cup, or snack. Early dental care supports more than healthy teeth; it helps prevent pain and supports overall health .

Book: Reading together during your child's bedtime routine does more than prepare them for sleep. It builds early literacy skills, strengthens your bond, and supports healthy brain development. Just a few minutes of reading each night can build your child's vocabulary and create calm, connected moments at the end of the day .

Bed: A regular bedtime routine helps children feel safe and secure. When bedtime happens around the same time each night, children are more likely to fall asleep faster, stay asleep longer, and wake up feeling rested. For toddlers and young children, a consistent routine can also help reduce common bedtime battles .

Tips for a Successful Bedtime Routine

Keep it Consistent: From the time you start winding down after dinner to the order in which you do things before bed (e.g., bath, then teeth, then a book), consistency is key. It helps your child know what to expect, making them feel more settled and secure at bedtime .

Create a Relaxing Atmosphere: Turn off screens and dim the lights 30 to 60 minutes before bedtime. The AAP recommends stopping the use of electronic devices before bed to help the mind wind down. Reading a story or singing a lullaby are great alternatives .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my child's sleep problems?

If your child has consistent trouble falling or staying asleep, snores loudly, has pauses in breathing during sleep, or shows signs of extreme daytime sleepiness, call your pediatrician. These can be signs of a sleep disorder that needs medical evaluation.

Q: How long does a bedtime routine take?

A typical bedtime routine can take about 20 to 45 minutes. The exact time will vary based on your child's age and the specific steps you include (like a bath, reading, and singing).

Q: What home remedies help with bedtime resistance?

Establishing a consistent, calm routine is the best home remedy. Also, ensure your child gets enough physical activity during the day and avoid screens for at least an hour before bed. If your child is scared of the dark, a nightlight may help.

Q: At what age should I start a bedtime routine?

You can start a bedtime routine as early as 6 months of age. Even for newborns, establishing a simple and consistent routine (such as a bath, massage, and lullaby) can be beneficial.

Q: Is it normal for my child to fight bedtime?

Yes, it's a common challenge for many parents. Toddlers often resist bedtime to test boundaries or because they don't want to miss out on anything. A consistent, positive routine is the best way to address this.

Q: How important is it to have a consistent bedtime?

Very important. A consistent bedtime helps set your child's internal body clock, making it easier for them to fall asleep at the same time each night and wake up feeling rested.

Q: When should I go to the ER for a sleep issue?

Go to the ER immediately if your child has trouble breathing during sleep, turns blue or grey, or is difficult to wake. These are emergency signs. For other sleep concerns, contact your pediatrician.
