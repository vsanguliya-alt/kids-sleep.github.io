Screen Time Before Bed for Your 6-Month-Old: What You Need to Know

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends no screen time for babies under 18 months, except for video chatting with relatives . Even &quot;background&quot; TV, where a screen is on in the room, can be distracting and negatively affect parent-child interactions and the quality of play . For babies under 2, screen use is discouraged entirely.

How Screen Time Disrupts Sleep

The blue light emitted by screens interferes with the production of melatonin, the hormone that regulates sleep-wake cycles. This makes it harder for your baby to fall asleep and stay asleep.

While a 6-month-old&#x27;s sleep is still developing, the principles of good sleep hygiene apply. The AAP recommends that screens be turned off at least one hour before bedtime to ensure a good night&#x27;s sleep.

Why This Matters for a 6-Month-Old

Critical brain development – at 6 months, your baby&#x27;s brain is developing rapidly, and they learn best through real-world interactions.

Parent-child bonding – face-to-face time with you is essential for healthy development.

Sleep associations – if your baby associates screens with falling asleep, they may need that same stimulation to get back to sleep during natural night wakings.

How to Create a Screen-Free Wind-Down Routine

Instead of screen time, focus on establishing a calming bedtime routine that helps your baby settle. A consistent, predictable routine helps signal that it&#x27;s time to sleep. A 20minute routine might include:

A warm bath

A gentle massage

Quiet cuddles

A lullaby or soft song

A short story (with a physical book, not a tablet)

Other Factors That Impact Sleep

Screen time is just one part of the sleep puzzle. It is also important to address other factors that contribute to a good night&#x27;s sleep:

Consistent sleep schedule – bed and wake times around the same time each day.

Room environment – keep the bedroom dark, cool (around 16°C), and quiet.

Safe sleep – place your baby on their back in a crib or bassinet with a firm mattress.

What to Do If Screen Time Has Been a Habit

If your baby is used to seeing a screen before bed, a sudden stop might be challenging. Consider these tips:

Gradually reduce screen time – move the screen time earlier in the evening and reduce the duration gradually.

Replace with a calming activity – transition from screen time to a bath or reading a book.

Be consistent – a calm, structured bedtime routine is the most effective tool for helping your baby get the rest they need.

Remember: The safest place for your baby to sleep is on their back in their own crib or bassinet, in your room for the first 6 months, with no soft objects, pillows, or loose bedding.
