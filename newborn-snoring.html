Newborn Sleep Training: What Parents Need to Know

The concept of "sleep training" is typically applied to older babies, not newborns. In the early weeks, a newborn's sleep patterns are naturally irregular, and they have a biological need to feed frequently.

Why Sleep Training Is Not Recommended for Newborns

Newborns have very different sleep needs compared to older babies. They sleep in short bursts, wake often to feed, and have not yet developed a regular day-night rhythm. Sleep training methods—such as controlled crying or extinction—are not appropriate for babies under 4months.

Your baby's tummy is tiny, so they need to feed frequently—roughly every 2 to 3 hours. This feeding requirement naturally determines their sleep schedule in the early weeks. Attempting to impose a strict sleep schedule or sleep training method on a newborn is not developmentally appropriate and can be harmful.

What to Do Instead

Instead of sleep training, focus on responding to your newborn's needs and establishing healthy sleep habits gradually.

Watch for sleep cues. Yawning, eye-rubbing, and fussiness are signs it's time for a nap. After being awake for about 1 to 2 hours, most newborns show signs that they are ready for sleep.

Create a calming sleep environment. Keep the room dark and quiet, and use a comfortable temperature. A calming, low-stimulation environment can help your baby settle more easily.

Develop a consistent bedtime routine. A predictable routine helps signal that it's time to sleep. This could include a warm bath, a gentle massage, a short story, or a lullaby.

Practice safe sleep. Always place your baby on their back to sleep. Use a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet, and keep the sleep area clear of soft objects, pillows, and loose bedding.

When to See a Doctor

If you are worried about your newborn's sleep patterns or behavior, you should contact your healthcare provider. It is important to know the warning signs for more serious problems.

Trust your instincts. If you are worried about your newborn, call your health care provider right away.
