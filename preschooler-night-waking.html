Preschooler Night Waking: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Night waking is a common sleep problem in preschoolers, affecting a significant number of young children . If your 3- to 5-year-old is suddenly waking up during the night, you are not alone.

What Is Normal?

At preschool age, a child typically needs about 10 to 13 hours of sleep in a 24-hour period, often split between nighttime and a daytime nap . However, waking up during the night is normal. All children (and adults) experience brief arousals between sleep cycles; a child who wakes and cries out may simply not have learned to fall back asleep on their own .

Common Causes of Night Waking

Several factors can contribute to your child&#x27;s disrupted sleep:

Behavioral Sleep Problems

Many children develop &quot;inappropriate sleep onset associations&quot;—they learn to rely on being rocked or having a parent present to fall asleep. When they wake at night, they can&#x27;t settle back down without that same support . This is often called behavioral insomnia of childhood .

Night Terrors vs. Nightmares

It&#x27;s helpful to distinguish these two events :

Feature Night Terrors Nightmares

Timing Usually first third of the night (often between midnight and 2 a.m.) Typically later in the night/early morning

Child&#x27;s State Appears awake but is asleep; disoriented and confused Fully wakes up, alert, and scared

Memory No memory of the event the next morning Can often remember the scary dream

Duration 10minutes, then returns to sleep Variable

Night terrors are most common in children ages 3 through 7 and may be triggered by fever, lack of sleep, or emotional stress.

Other Physical Causes

Consider ruling out:

Allergies: Frequent nocturnal awakening has been associated with allergic rhinitis in preschoolers.

Pain or discomfort: Earaches, teething, or other physical issues.

Snoring: Can indicate sleep-disordered breathing.

What You Can Do

Rule out physical causes first. Does your child snore? Are they itchy or in pain? .

Re-establish a consistent bedtime routine. A predictable routine helps signal that it&#x27;s time to sleep .

Avoid screens and stimulating activities before bed .

For sleep associations, gradually teach your child to fall asleep independently. This may involve &quot;extinction&quot; or &quot;positive routines&quot; approaches .

Manage night terrors: Do not try to wake your child. Stay calm and keep them safe; they will usually settle back to sleep within 10minutes .

When to See a Doctor

Contact your pediatrician if night terrors occur often, disrupt sleep on a regular basis, cause injury, or are accompanied by other concerning symptoms .

Trust your instincts. If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider right away.
