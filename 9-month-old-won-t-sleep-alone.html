Why Won&#x27;t My 9-Month-Old Sleep Alone? Understanding and Managing It

It is very common for a 9-month-old to resist sleeping alone. This phase is often tied to a normal developmental stage called separation anxiety.

Separation Anxiety and Sleep

At around 9 months, babies begin to develop a stronger awareness of the world and may start to understand that when a parent leaves the room, they are still nearby but out of sight. This can lead to a fear of being separated from their parents, which often manifests at bedtime and during night wakings. They may become more clingy, crying or calling out when you leave the room or put them to bed. This can lead to:

Refusing to Sleep Alone: The simple act of you leaving can cause real distress.

Frequent Night Wakings: Your baby may wake up and need reassurance that you are still nearby.

Bedtime Battles: Resistance to going to sleep.

How to Help Your Baby Sleep Alone

Here are some gentle, evidence-based strategies to help your baby through this phase:

Stick to a Predictable Routine: A consistent, calming bedtime routine is your best tool. It provides a sense of security and predictability when your baby is feeling anxious.

Provide a Comfort Object: A small, safe soft toy or blanket can act as a &quot;transitional object,&quot; offering comfort and a sense of security when you are not there. This is only appropriate if your baby is developmentally ready to have an object in the crib.

Acknowledge Their Fear: Validate their feelings. If they are scared of a monster, check the room together (with the lights off) and prove it&#x27;s safe. Use a small, dim night-light to help reduce any fear of the dark.

Practice Being Apart: During the day, practice short separations. Tell your baby you are going to another room and will be right back. Always return as promised to build trust.

Be Calm and Reassuring: If they call for you, go in and offer comfort, but try to make your visits brief and calm. Avoid creating new habits, like getting into bed with them. You can pat their back, tell them it&#x27;s time to sleep, and leave them awake in their bed.

Important Safety Considerations

During this phase, it is vital to follow safe sleep guidelines to reduce the risk of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS). Always place your baby on their back to sleep, in a separate crib or bassinet in your room, and keep the crib clear of soft objects and loose bedding.

When to Seek Help

While this phase is normal, if the symptoms are severe and last longer than a few weeks, or if you are worried about your baby&#x27;s development, speak to your pediatrician. They can help rule out any underlying issues and offer personalized guidance.
