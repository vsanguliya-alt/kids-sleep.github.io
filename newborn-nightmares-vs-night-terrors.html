Nightmares vs. Night Terrors in Newborns: What Parents Need to Know

It can be worrying to see your newborn suddenly cry or appear distressed during sleep. However, the behavior you are witnessing is unlikely to be a nightmare or a night terror, as these conditions typically affect older children.

Nightmares vs. Night Terrors: An Overview

The main differences between a nightmare and a night terror lie in when they happen, how your child behaves, and what they remember afterward.

Nightmares are essentially bad dreams. They occur later in the night, during the REM (Rapid Eye Movement) stage of sleep when dreaming is most active. If a child has a nightmare, they will usually wake up fully, feel frightened, and be able to recall at least part of the scary dream. They will seek comfort and are typically easy to console.

Night Terrors, on the other hand, are not dreams at all. They are a type of sleep disorder called a parasomnia that occurs during non-REM, deep sleep. This stage is typically in the first half of the night. During a night terror, a child may sit up in bed, scream, thrash around, and appear terrified, but they are not fully awake.

Why Newborns Are Different

It is important to understand that both nightmares and night terrors are typically seen in children and adults, not newborns. Night terrors are considered a sign of a developing nervous system, which is why they are more common in children, as their brains are still developing. Newborns do not have fully formed sleep cycles or the cognitive ability to experience nightmares.

What Looks Like a Nightmare in a Newborn?

What you might be witnessing in your newborn is simply a normal part of their sleep development. Newborns sleep in short bursts, often cycling through REM sleep. They may fuss, cry, or move during sleep, and these movements are usually not a sign of distress but rather a normal part of the sleep process.
