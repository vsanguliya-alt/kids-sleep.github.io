1 Year Old Sleep Apnea Signs: 7 Signs Parents Should Know

Pediatric obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a condition in which a child's breathing is partly or completely blocked during sleep . It is a common disorder that causes breathing to stop or become very shallow during sleep . Untreated sleep apnea can affect a child's growth, quality of life, learning, behavior, and mood . Here are 7 key signs that may indicate your 1-year-old has sleep apnea.

1. Snoring

Children who have sleep apnea nearly always snore . However, not all children who snore have sleep apnea—snoring is a common clue, but it must be evaluated alongside other signs .

2. Pauses in Breathing

Gasping, choking, or noisy breathing that may be worse when your child is on his or her back can be a sign . Long silent pauses in breathing followed by snorts, choking, and gasps for air are classic symptoms .

3. Restless Sleep

Waking up a lot, restless tossing and turning, and frequent awakenings from sleep are common signs . Your child may also sweat during sleep .

4. Mouth Breathing

Breathing mainly through the mouth while awake or asleep is a common symptom in younger children .

5. Bedwetting

Bedwetting that starts after a long period of being dry overnight can be a sign of sleep apnea in children .

6. Unusual Sleep Position

Children with sleep apnea may sleep in unusual positions, such as seated, with an arched back, or with the head tilted back .

7. Daytime Problems

Sleep apnea can cause difficulties during the day, including:

Irritability and moodiness: Acting grumpy, cranky, or irritable Hyperactivity and behavior problems: Acting hyper, impulsive, or aggressive Attention problems: Difficulty paying attention Fatigue and sleepiness: Extreme tiredness, falling asleep at unusual times, or napping more than expected When to See a Doctor

If your child has two or more of these symptoms, particularly snoring with pauses in breathing, talk to your pediatrician. Your doctor may recommend a sleep study (polysomnography) to diagnose sleep apnea . Early detection and treatment can prevent serious complications .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor for my 1-year-old's sleep apnea?

Call your pediatrician if your child snores loudly at least 3 nights a week, has pauses in breathing during sleep, or exhibits gasping, choking, or restless sleep . Daytime signs like irritability, hyperactivity, or extreme tiredness are also reasons to seek evaluation .

Q: How long does sleep apnea last in a 1-year-old?

Sleep apnea is a chronic condition that does not resolve on its own. It requires medical evaluation and treatment . With appropriate treatment, symptoms can improve significantly .

Q: What home remedies help a 1-year-old with sleep apnea?

There are no home remedies for sleep apnea—it requires medical evaluation. Treatment may include surgery to remove tonsils and adenoids (adenotonsillectomy) for children over 2 years old, or other interventions like positive airway pressure (PAP) therapy .

Q: Is snoring normal in a 1-year-old?

Snoring is not normal in children. While occasional soft snoring may occur with a cold, loud snoring that occurs regularly (at least 3 nights a week) is a red flag for sleep apnea .

Q: Does sleep apnea affect a 1-year-old's development?

Yes. Untreated sleep apnea can cause serious problems including neurobehavioral issues, cardiovascular problems, and impairment of growth and development . Early detection and treatment are important .

Q: When should I go to the ER for my 1-year-old with sleep apnea?

Go to the ER immediately if your child has trouble breathing, turns blue or grey, or is difficult to wake. These are emergency signs. For other concerns, contact your pediatrician .

Q: Can a 1-year-old have sleep apnea without snoring?

Yes. Infants and young children with obstructive sleep apnea don't always snore—they might just have disturbed sleep . This is why other signs like restless sleep, mouth breathing, and daytime behavior changes are important to watch for .
