Gentle Sleep Training Methods for Your 6-Month-Old

The 6-month mark is often seen as a turning point for infant sleep. Many babies are now developmentally ready to learn the skill of self-soothing and falling asleep independently, with research supporting sleep training for this age group .

However, it&#x27;s a deeply personal decision, and there&#x27;s no single &quot;right&quot; way. Before starting, ensure you&#x27;re following safe sleep guidelines: place your baby on their back in a cot free of soft toys, pillows, and loose bedding .

Setting the Foundation

Before choosing a specific method, establish a strong foundation for sleep:

Consistent Bedtime Routine

A predictable, calming routine (bath, story, cuddle) lasting 20–30 minutes signals sleep time and is associated with better sleep outcomes .

Day-Night Awareness

Keep the environment bright and active during the day, and dim and quiet at night to help regulate your baby&#x27;s circadian rhythm .

Drowsy but Awake

Placing your baby in their cot while sleepy but still awake is one of the most important principles. It allows them to practice falling asleep in their own sleep space without relying on being held or fed .

Gentle Sleep Training Approaches

If your 6-month-old is ready, several gentle methods can teach independent sleep without excessive crying.

The Chair Method (Gradual Withdrawal)

A parental fading technique where you sit near the cot, offering calm reassurance without picking your baby up. Each night, you move the chair farther away until you&#x27;re out of the room . This method is gentle and effective .

Pick Up / Put Down

If your baby cries, gently pick them up for comfort. Once calm, place them back in the cot. Repeat as needed. Over time, your baby learns they are supported while also learning to settle in their own sleep space .

The Shuffle Method

Popularised by Kim West, this method gradually reduces parental soothing, allowing babies to slowly develop independent sleep skills while parents remain present .

Responsive Settling

Place your baby on their back awake and watch for drowsiness. Use a calm voice and reassuring words. If they can&#x27;t settle, try gentle &quot;shhh&quot; sounds, rhythmic patting, or rocking. If they become distressed, pick them up for comfort and try again .

Modified Extinction (Ferber Method)

This method involves doing timed check-ins when your baby cries, gradually increasing the intervals between visits. You don&#x27;t pick them up but offer brief reassurance (a few words, gentle pats) and leave . Some find it works relatively quickly , but others may find the crying emotionally difficult .

What to Consider

Crying: Most sleep training methods involve some crying. Avoid it if your baby is unwell, hungry, has a dirty diaper, or if there are major changes (travel, illness) .

Effectiveness: While sleep training can improve parent-reported sleep , studies show sleep-trained babies may wake just as often according to objective measures . The benefits may be temporary .

Consistency: Stick with one approach for at least 21–30 days to allow your baby to learn .

Consult Your Pediatrician: Always talk to your child&#x27;s healthcare provider before starting any sleep changes to ensure your baby is ready .
