Nightmares vs. Night Terrors in Babies: A Guide for Parents

Waking to your baby's sudden, terrified screams can be alarming. However, the behavior you are witnessing is likely either a nightmare or a night terror, two very different sleep events. Understanding the distinction is the key to knowing how to respond effectively.

Nightmares vs. Night Terrors: Key Differences

The main differences between a nightmare and a night terror lie in when they happen, how your child behaves, and what they remember afterward.

Nightmares are essentially bad dreams. They occur later in the night, during the REM (Rapid Eye Movement) stage of sleep when dreaming is most active . If your child has a nightmare, they will usually wake up fully, feel frightened, and be able to recall at least part of the scary dream . They will seek comfort from you and are typically easy to console .

Night Terrors, on the other hand, are not dreams at all. They are a type of sleep disorder called a parasomnia that occurs during non-REM, deep sleep . This stage is typically in the first half of the night, often 1 to 3 hours after falling asleep .

During a Nightmare: A nightmare is a frightening dream that your child will wake up from. In this case, your presence is the best remedy. Hold them, reassure them that they are safe, and stay with them until they feel calm enough to fall back asleep .

During a Night Terror: During an episode, do not attempt to wake your child, as this can prolong or intensify the episode . Instead, stay calm and stay nearby to ensure their safety; the episode will usually pass within 10minutes .

Prevention and When to See a Doctor

Night terrors can be unsettling to witness, but they are generally harmless and most children outgrow them . They can be triggered by overtiredness, illness, or stress . A consistent, calming bedtime routine and ensuring your child gets enough sleep can help prevent them .

Trust your instincts. Speak with your pediatrician if the episodes become very frequent, occur more than twice a week, or regularly disrupt sleep .
