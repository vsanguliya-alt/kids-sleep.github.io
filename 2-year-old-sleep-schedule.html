2-Year-Old Sleep Schedule: A Guide for Parents

At two years old, your child&#x27;s sleep is likely becoming more predictable, but it&#x27;s also an age where nap transitions can cause some disruption. Understanding your toddler&#x27;s sleep needs is the first step to creating a schedule that works.

How Much Sleep Does a 2-Year-Old Need?

A two-year-old needs about 11 to 14 hours of good quality sleep every 24 hours, which includes their nighttime sleep and a daytime nap . This is usually made up of 10 to 12 hours at night, with the rest coming from a 1 to 2-hour nap during the day .

The Ideal Sleep Schedule for a 2-Year-Old

A typical and healthy sleep schedule for a 2-year-old might look something like this :

Wake up: 6:30 AM

Nap: 12:30 PM – 2:30 PM

Bedtime: 7:30 PM

It&#x27;s important to remember that this is just an example. A loose schedule that works for your family is perfectly acceptable, as long as your child is getting enough total sleep . A 2-year-old generally needs about 10 to 11 hours of sleep at night and up to 2 hours of sleep during the day . This means if your child wakes up at 6:00 AM, their bedtime should be between 7:00 and 8:00 PM. If their nap ends around 2:30 PM, you need to set a routine to get them ready for bed at 7:00 PM .

How to Tell if the Schedule Needs Adjusting

A 2-year-old&#x27;s sleep needs can change. Common signs that your child&#x27;s schedule might need tweaking include :

They have a hard time falling asleep at nap time.

They refuse to sleep when their normal bedtime rolls around.

They consistently fight their nap or seem energetic during their usual nap time.

They are waking up too early in the morning.

If you notice these signs, there are strategies you can try. For example, if a nap is interfering with bedtime, you can try shortening the nap or moving it earlier in the day. A consistent nap wake-up time that is at least three to four hours before bedtime can be very helpful .

Safe Sleep Environment

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends that babies be placed on their backs to sleep, even for short naps . They should sleep on a firm sleep surface, and soft objects and loose bedding should be kept out of the crib . Making sure that your baby doesn&#x27;t get too hot is also a key part of safe sleep .
