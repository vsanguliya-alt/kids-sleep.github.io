Screen Time for Infants Before Bed: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Putting a baby to bed after screen time can be a common habit, but evidence suggests this practice may disrupt their sleep and overall development. Major health organizations offer clear guidelines for avoiding screen time in infancy, especially before sleep.

🔬 Why Screen Time Before Bed Is Discouraged

The primary concern is the effect of blue light, which can suppress the production of melatonin—the hormone that regulates sleep-wake cycles—making it harder for a baby to fall asleep . Additionally, the bright colors and rapid movement of on-screen content can be overstimulating for a young child&#x27;s developing brain . A study of infants and toddlers found that pre-sleep screen time was significantly associated with poorer parent-reported sleep quality . Another study showed that removing screen time in the hour before bed led to improvements in toddler sleep, including better sleep efficiency and fewer night awakenings .

📱 Official Recommendations for Screen Time

Based on guidelines from the World Health Organization (WHO) and the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP):

For infants under 2 years old: Screen time is generally not recommended, with the sole exception of supervised video chatting with family and friends .

For children aged 2 to 5 years: Sedentary screen time should be limited to one hour per day of high-quality, educational content, ideally watched together with a caregiver .

🌙 Practical Steps for a Screen-Free Bedtime

To support healthy sleep, create a calm wind-down routine without screens:

No screens one hour before bed: This is a key recommendation to protect sleep .

Establish a screen-free bedtime routine: Activities like reading a story, singing a lullaby, or a warm bath are more beneficial than screen time .

Make the bedroom a screen-free zone: Keep televisions, tablets, and phones out of the baby&#x27;s room .

Model healthy habits: Your own screen use matters. Babies learn through interaction and connection with you . Reducing your own device use around your child supports their development and strengthens your bond .
