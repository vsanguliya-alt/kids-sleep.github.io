Bedtime Routine for a 1-Year-Old: A Parent's Guide

A consistent bedtime routine is one of the most effective ways to help your 1-year-old get the sleep they need for healthy growth and development. Children thrive on predictability, and a regular routine helps signal that it is time to wind down and prepare for sleep.

Why a Bedtime Routine Matters

At 1 year old, your child is reaching exciting developmental milestones in gross motor, fine motor, language, and social skills . A predictable routine can help them feel secure and transition more easily from active play to a calm state, which is essential for quality sleep.

Key Steps for a Successful Bedtime Routine

Keep it consistent. Doing the same activities in the same order each night helps your child know what to expect. A simple 20minute routine might include a warm bath, brushing teeth, putting on pajamas, reading a story, and saying goodnight.

Start winding down early. Begin the routine at the same time each night, about 30 to 60 minutes before you want your child to be asleep. This gives them time to transition from active play to a calmer state.

Create a sleep-friendly environment. Make sure your child's bedroom is cool, dark, and quiet. Consider using blackout curtains and a white noise machine if needed.

Include calming activities. Reading a book, singing a lullaby, or talking quietly about the day can help your child relax. Avoid stimulating activities like roughhousing, scary stories, or screen time in the hour before bed.

Follow the same sequence every night. If your child knows that bath time is followed by a story and then lights out, they are more likely to accept bedtime as a natural part of the day.

Additional Sleep Tips

Avoid large meals and caffeine close to bedtime.

Keep a consistent wake-up time, even on weekends.

Use the bed only for sleep (and not for play or time-outs).

Exercise regularly, but not too close to bedtime.

If Bedtime Is a Struggle

It is normal for children to resist bedtime occasionally. If your child calls out or gets out of bed, stay calm and return them to bed with minimal interaction. Consistency and patience are key. Over time, a predictable routine will help your child settle more easily.

When to Talk to Your Doctor

Well-child visits are important to your toddler's health, and routine exams and screenings help you and your kids prevent and treat health problems as well as chart their growth and development . If you have concerns about your child's sleep, discuss them with your pediatrician.
