Co-Sleeping Safety for Your 9-Month-Old

At 9 months old, your baby is growing fast, and it's natural to wonder about safe sleep arrangements. The short answer is: room-sharing is recommended, but bed-sharing is not.

Room-Sharing vs. Bed-Sharing: What's the Difference?

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) makes a clear distinction between these two terms .

Room-Sharing means your baby sleeps in the same room as you, but in their own separate, safe sleep space, such as a crib, bassinet, or play yard .

Bed-Sharing means your baby sleeps on the same surface as an adult, such as a bed, couch, or chair .

The AAP Recommendation: Room-Sharing is Best

The AAP strongly recommends room-sharing without bed-sharing for at least the first 6 months of life, and ideally for the first full year . In fact, room-sharing can reduce the risk of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) by as much as 50% .

This arrangement keeps your baby close for feeding, comforting, and monitoring, but on a separate, safer surface . Keeping your baby in the same room as you for the first 6 to 12 months is also recommended by organizations like The Lullaby Trust to reduce the risk of SIDS .

Why Bed-Sharing is Not Recommended for 9-Month-Olds

While the risk of SIDS is highest in the first 4 months, the AAP and other experts still strongly discourage bed-sharing for infants under 1 year old . The primary risks include:

Suffocation: An adult bed has pillows, soft mattresses, and loose bedding that can obstruct a baby's airway .

Entrapment: A baby can get wedged and suffocate between a mattress and a wall, headboard, or other object .

Overheating: Adult beds and bedding can cause a baby to overheat, which is a risk factor for SIDS .

Adult Rolling: A parent can accidentally roll onto a baby during sleep .

If You Choose to Bed-Share

Some parents may decide to bed-share despite the risks. While it is not recommended, if you choose to do so, it's critical to take steps to make the environment as safe as possible .

Wait until your baby is over six months old .

Never bed-share if you or your partner: are excessively tired, have consumed alcohol, are a smoker, or have taken any drugs (prescription or otherwise) that make you sleepy .

Never bed-share if your baby: is ill or has a fever .

Never bed-share on a sofa or armchair. This is extremely dangerous .

Place your baby on their back to sleep .

Use a firm mattress and keep the sleep area clear of pillows, soft toys, and loose bedding .

Make sure your baby won't fall out of bed or get trapped between the mattress and the wall .

Never leave your baby alone in an adult bed .

Ultimately, the safest place for your 9-month-old to sleep is in their own crib or bassinet, in the same room as you .
