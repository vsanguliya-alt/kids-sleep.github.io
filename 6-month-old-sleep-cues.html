Recognizing Your 6-Month-Old's Sleep Cues

At 6 months old, your baby is becoming more aware of their surroundings, which can sometimes make their sleep cues harder to recognize. However, learning to spot these signs is key to preventing overtiredness, which can make it much harder for a baby to fall asleep . Here are the most common cues that your 6-month-old is ready for a nap or bedtime.

🧸 Common Signs of Tiredness

Your baby may be getting tired if you observe any of these behaviors :

Clinginess and Demands for Attention: A baby who is tired may become unusually needy, wanting to be held constantly .

Fussiness and Irritability: A tired 6-month-old can quickly become easily frustrated, irritable, or whiny. They may also be fussy during feeding .

Yawning and Eye Rubbing: Like adults, babies often yawn or rub their eyes when they are tired. Heavy, droopy eyelids or a glazed-over expression are other facial cues .

Losing Interest in Play: If your baby is usually engaged with toys but suddenly seems uninterested or turns away, this is a key sleep cue .

Arching Back: Overtiredness can cause a baby to arch their back or stiffen their body .

Jerky Movements: A baby who is overtired may startle easily and have jerky, uncoordinated movements.

🛏️ What to Do When You Spot Sleep Cues

When you notice these signs, it's time to start a calming wind-down routine. Reducing stimulation by putting toys away, dimming the lights, and speaking in a quiet, calm voice can help signal to your baby that sleep time is approaching .

Acting quickly and putting your baby down for a nap when they are drowsy but still awake can make a significant difference. This approach helps them learn to self-soothe and fall asleep independently, rather than relying on you to rock or cuddle them to sleep .
